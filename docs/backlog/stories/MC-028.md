## Context

A **panel gutter** is the white or black space between panels, running
horizontally across the strip, with speech bubbles routinely sitting on top of
one (`docs/wiki/architecture.md`, "Two kinds of blank space"). The user's
hand-marked corpus rects bound a panel between two of them. Nothing in
`cropper-core` locates one, and MC-019's accuracy bar is unreachable until
something does or until the bar changes.

**This spike exists because two feature stories have now guessed at the row
axis and both were measured wrong afterwards.** MC-025 shipped a row locator
that moved the corpus by zero entries; MC-026 was planned around an
interior-band rule whose ceiling on the row axis was 5 of 21. The deliverable
here is a **measured yes or no**, not code.

### Where to start, so this does not retrace ground

Four measurements are already taken and are recorded with their numbers in
MC-026's `## Notes`. **Read them before designing anything**; re-running one is
fine, re-discovering one is waste.

1. **Finding 4 - the panel gutter is separable.** Per-row **flat fraction** -
   the share of a row's pixels within `uniform_tolerance` of that row's
   *median*, which is `content.rs`'s `flat_fraction` per line instead of per
   strip - has median **0.201** inside a panel and **0.984** in the gutter band
   just outside the mark. One threshold at 0.40 classifies **61 of 63 bands**
   correctly. Failures: `Screenshot (103)` below (0.247, the white diagonal
   gutter) and `Screenshot (93)` above (0.545, borderline throughout).
2. **Finding 5 - it is not locatable by a search over that profile.** Threshold
   {0.35, 0.40, 0.45, 0.50, 0.60} x minimum gutter depth {1, 4, 8, 16} x
   selector {widest, nearest-centre, holds-centre}, columns given for free:
   **0 of 21 right, 17 to 20 clips in every combination**. Restricting a gutter
   row to a near-black or near-white median gives **0 of 21, 17 to 18 clips**.
3. **The failure mode is the opposite of MC-025's, and it is the most useful
   fact here.** MC-025 found *no* boundary; this finds *too many*. The clips
   mean the search cut **inside** the panel, because panels contain flat
   full-width rows - open sky, a dark field, a speech bubble spanning the width
   - that a single row profile cannot tell from a gutter.
4. **Mean absolute deviation is dead on this axis** and so is the earlier flat
   fraction attempt: 5 of 21 and 8 of 21 respectively (MC-026 findings 1 and
   1b). A third proposal has to beat those rather than re-run them.

### Candidate directions, offered and not required

The missing ingredient in finding 5 is **context the single-row profile does
not carry**. Three places to look, in no particular order, and a fourth rule is
just as welcome:

- **page background tone** - what the flat tone of this page's gutters actually
  is, established once per image rather than assumed to be near-black or
  near-white;
- **full-width tone consistency** - whether a flat run holds *one* tone across
  the whole page width, which a speech bubble over a gutter does and a flat
  patch of sky inside a panel may not;
- **column structure** - how a candidate run relates to the page column MC-027
  locates, which is real information the row search has never been given.

### The awkward file, and the user's ruling on it

`Screenshot (103).jpg` has black above the mark (not strictly a gutter, but to
be treated as one) and a **white diagonal gutter** below. The user has said it
is **explicitly not a priority**, and that including the region above the mark
is acceptable if necessary. Do not let it drive the design; report what it does.

## Acceptance criteria

The deliverable is one document, `docs/wiki/panel-gutter-search.md`, and these
criteria are about it.

- **AC-1** — *The question is answered either way.* Given the investigation has ended, when the document is read, then it states one of exactly two verdicts in its first section: **a recommended rule**, with its corpus score and its clip count under MC-019's window; or **a reasoned negative** - no rule tried reaches the bar - with the alternatives tried and why each failed. **A negative is a real, acceptable and valuable outcome of this story.** It is not a failure to deliver, it is the answer that stops a third feature story from guessing, and it sends the bar itself back to the user as an MC-019 `## Amendments` question.
- **AC-2** — *The score is stated the way MC-019 will measure it.* Given either verdict, when the document reports a number, then it reports **both** of MC-019's numbers for every rule it scores: how many of the 21 crop entries land inside the expected rect expanded by `margin_px + 8` px, **and** how many are clipped (a produced rect that fails to contain the expected rect). A rule that scores well and clips is a rule that has failed, because MC-019 AC-1 ranks a clip above every other defect and MC-005 decision 13 is the reason.
- **AC-3** — *The four measurements are not re-run as discoveries.* Given the document, when its evidence section is read, then it begins from MC-026's findings 4, 5 and 1b by reference, and every rule it proposes is compared against those three baselines - 5 of 21, 8 of 21, and 0 of 21 with 17 to 20 clips. A rule that does not beat them is reported as not beating them.
- **AC-4** — *The negative controls are stated.* Given any rule the document recommends or scores, when its section is read, then it names the inputs on which the rule must **not** fire and what it scores on them: at minimum a panel containing a flat full-width row (the defect finding 5 names) and the seven `"expect": "flag"` corpus entries, which must not become croppable. A rule with no control is reported as having none.
- **AC-5** — *Every number is reproducible.* Given the document, when a number in it is questioned, then the document names the command that produced it and the location of the harness, as MC-025's and MC-026's `## Notes` do. No number appears without one.
- **AC-6** — *The next story is specified or explicitly not needed.* Given a recommended rule, when the document ends, then it states the shape of the implementation story that would carry it: which module, which `Tuning` field if any, the required gate that would fail if it broke, and whether it fits one RED -> GREEN cycle. Given a reasoned negative, it states instead which of MC-019's numbers has to move - the manifest, the 11 px tolerance, or the 90% - and what evidence the user would need to decide.

- **Oracle-free, and the whole of the work**: every rule proposed, every threshold in it, and the metric each is scored by. The corpus is the oracle and a clip is the loss function.
- **Mechanical**: AC-2, AC-5, AC-6 - the reporting shape.

**Success condition, which can come out either way.** This spike succeeds if,
at the end, a reader can say which of these is true and why, with numbers: *a
rule exists that reaches MC-019's window on at least 20 of 21 with zero clips*;
or *no rule tried reaches it, and here are the five best, their scores, their
clip counts and the reason each fails*. It fails only if it comes back with
neither - a rule with no score, or a negative with no alternatives tried.

**Timebox.** If ten rules have been scored and none beats 8 of 21, that is the
reasoned negative; write it and stop. The value of this story is highest when it
returns "no" early.

### The instrument was validated before anything new was measured

MC-026 finding 4 reproduced from its definition, in this story's own harness,
over `codec::to_luma`'s plane rather than MC-026's float BT.601 rounding:

```
flat fraction: inside a panel 0.201   in the gutter band outside 0.984
best single threshold: 0.40 classifies 61/63 bands correctly
```

Identical to the recorded numbers, and agreeing with MC-026's own `gutter`
binary to three decimals on all 63 bands. Command:
`cargo run --bin repro`.

**One correction to MC-026's prose, which its conclusion survives.** MC-026's
`## Notes` names the two bands its 0.40 threshold misclassifies as
"`Screenshot (103)` below (0.247 ...) and `Screenshot (93)` above (0.545,
borderline throughout)". 0.545 is on the correct side of 0.40, so that band is
classified *right*; the second failure is **`2026-01-05 13_49_39.png` above, at
0.294**. Verified two ways - this harness, and MC-026's own binary re-run, which
prints `13_49_39 ... 0.294` for that band. `Screenshot (93)` is genuinely the

### The starting point that was new, and the three candidate directions

MC-026 findings 1, 1b and 5 profiled rows over the **marked** columns and over
the **whole image's** rows. Every rule here is profiled over the **page column
MC-027 locates**, inside the rect the real pipeline produces - finding 7's move
applied to the other axis. All three of the story's candidate directions were
taken:

1. **page background tone** - every selector is given the page's own flat tone,
   the modal row median over flat rows in 8-level bins, established once per
   image. Best 4 of 21.
2. **full-width tone consistency** - flat runs are split into tone bands, and
   three of the six selectors place the edge at a band boundary rather than at
   the flat region's. This is where the best top-edge score comes from
   (13 of 21) and it is also where the clips come from.
3. **column structure** - `cargo run --bin marginrow`, the pixels *beside* the
   page column read one row at a time. **0 of 21 at every one of 48
   parameterisations**, and the mechanism is in the offsets: the page column is
   narrower than the browser's toolbars, so the pixels beside it are flat
   everywhere in the rect, chrome rows included. Measured and reported as
   failing.

### Why it fails, bounded rather than sampled

The two measurements that make this a negative about the *problem* rather than
about 264 guesses:

- **`cargo run --bin ceil`** - cherry-picking the best of all 216
  parameterisations **per file**, which no single rule can do, reaches
  **7 of 21**; fourteen entries are reachable by no member. No selector over
  these placements can reach the bar, whatever selector is invented.
- **`cargo run --bin feature` and `--bin ordinal`** - the marked edges are *at*
  pixel features: **40 of 42 within MC-019's 11 px window** of a change of one
  of sixteen kinds, and **30 of 42 exactly on one**. But each feature kind
  changes at 2 to 150 other rows in the same rect, and the ordinal of the right
  one runs k = 1..16 (flat 0.40), 3..11 (flat 0.90), 5..35 (tone), 6..30
  (gradient). Separating a gutter row from a panel row is a per-row question the
  flat fraction answers well (finding 4); locating the page is a *choice among
  dozens of correct separations*, and nothing in the profile ranks them.

### And a finding about the oracle, which is AC-6's answer

`cargo run --bin interior`. For each marked edge, the maximal homogeneous band
containing it - rows flat at 0.90 or above, each within `uniform_tolerance` of
the previous - and the edge's depth inside it:

```
uniform gutter the marked rects CONTAIN, over the 42 edges: min 0, median 0, max 50
edges where that depth exceeds MC-019's 11 px window: 6 of 42
all values: [0 x 30, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 12, 22, 23, 36, 41, 50]
```

Thirty edges sit exactly on a change, tight against the art as
`architecture.md` describes. Six sit 12 to 50 rows inside a homogeneous gutter
band, with 97 and 170 rows of the same band outside them. The decisive pair:
**`2025-10-14 23_30_20.png` and `2025-10-20 15_37_25.png` have the same
structure** - browser chrome to row 167, then flat white at tone 248-255, art
much lower - **and are marked at 171 and 228.** A 57 px disagreement between two
marks of one pixel feature, against an 11 px window.

That is not a criticism of the marking. A person boxing a page inside a uniform
white gutter has no reason to prefer one row to another and both boxes are
correct *as pages*. It does mean the manifest does not define the row edges to
11 px, so no rule can be fitted to them - which is why section 9 puts the
manifest, and not the 90 % or the tolerance, as the number that moves.

**`architecture.md` is affected and was deliberately not edited.** Its "Two
kinds of blank space" says the marked rects "bound a panel **between panel
gutters**; everything outside them vertically is panel gutter, not artwork".
That is true of 30 of the 42 edges and not of 6 of them, where the mark sits
well inside the gutter. Correcting it is a product statement about what the
marks mean, which is the user's to make, and `## Out of scope` keeps this story
off the manifest. Section 9 is where the question is put.

### The controls, and that they discriminate

AC-4 asks for the inputs a rule must **not** fire on, and a control that cannot
fire is not a control:

- **the metric control** - a synthetic 400 x 600 page with gutter at tone 250 in
  rows 0-60 and 540-600, textured art between, and a **flat full-width block at
  the same tone in rows 200-230, inside the panel**: exactly the defect finding
  5 names. It **fires for 6 of the 216 parameterisations** (every
  `DeepestGutterEnd` at a flat threshold of 0.40), printed by
  `cargo run --bin ceil`. The five best rules hold on it.
- **the corpus control** - the seven `"expect": "flag"` entries. Six are
  `Flagged` on this tree and `2025-02-27 22_46_15.png` is already `Cropped` (the
  entry MC-026's AC-4 names `NOT_THIS_STORYS_TO_FIX`), so **1 of 7 is the
  no-regression baseline**. Two of the five best rules break it - the
  `LastGutterEnd` pair at tone tolerance 24 makes **four** of the seven
  croppable - which is an independent reason to reject the widened tolerance
  that their scores otherwise argue for.
