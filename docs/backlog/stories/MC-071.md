### Why only four bottoms need ruling

MC-063 re-scoped its furniture ruling to "just the bottoms" on the user's
choice (MC-063 `## Notes`, 2026-09-30), and the same reasoning carries over
unchanged:
- **Tops:** every one of the 15 marks starts on a browser-bar end (115, 133
  or 167). The art touches the browser bar, so `T` is the mark's top and there
  is no site header above it.
- **Bottoms:** where a mark ends on its group's usual bottom row, the art
  touches the taskbar, so `B` is the mark's bottom.
- **The only unknown** is the band below a mark that stops short: page
  background, which a crop may keep, or furniture (MC-063 found it was the
  browser's horizontal scrollbar on four of its eight; the user ruled the
  scrollbar browser furniture).

Grouped by top row, from the frozen marks (counts and ids only; oracle data,
not a crop):

| Top | Usual bottom | Ending short |
|---|---|---|
| 133 (`Screenshot (N)`, mahwa panels) | 1392 (both) | none |
| 167 | 1400 (all 5) | none |
| 115 | 1400 (4 of 8) | `n02` 1399, `n06` 1392, `n10` 1392, `n15` 1399 |

So **11 are read from the mark and 4 are ruled** (Open question 2). What is
given up, as in MC-063: for the 11, an off-by-one in a mark would score as a
clip or as furniture present and would not be caught before AC-3.

**Consequence worth stating before the run.** The marks run the visible
height, so most entries' ceiling is tight: the crop must start exactly on the
browser-bar end and stop exactly on `B`, to the row. That is the predicate
MC-051 and MC-063 scored, exact to the row, and it is not loosened here: a
tolerance chosen now, knowing the marks, would be a threshold picked for this
set.

## Definitions used by every criterion

MC-063's, with the denominators changed:

- **Scored commit**: the `main` commit (or a story-branch commit whose
  `crates/`, `Cargo.toml` and `Cargo.lock` are identical to `main`'s) whose
  release build is run, recorded with the harness's source hash **before**
  AC-3 runs. `git diff 1438b2b <scored commit> -- crates/core
  crates/engine/src crates/app/src Cargo.lock --stat` is pasted beside it.
- **The crop**: `process_file`'s outcome at `Tuning::default()` (margin_px
  0, the app's own setting) on the scored commit, release build. For
  `Cropped { rect }`, rows `rect.y .. rect.y + rect.h` (bottom exclusive). For
  `Flagged`, the whole image. Margin 3 is not scored (Open question 5).
- **Clip**: the crop does not contain the marked rect on all four sides,
  columns included.
- **`T`**: the browser-bar end (the mark's top on all 15, as above; no site
  header). **`B`**: the smallest of the scrollbar start, the site-footer start
  and the taskbar start, the image height if none. "Ends" is the first row
  below a bar; "starts" is its first row.
- **Furniture-free**: `crop.y >= T` and `crop.y + crop.h <= B`, exact to the
  row. A one-row remnant is present.
- **Cause attribution**, fixed before any crop: browser chrome if `crop.y <
  T`; browser scrollbar if the crop's bottom is past `scrollbarStart`; reader
  footer if the crop's bottom is past `footerStart`; taskbar if the crop's
  bottom is past `taskbarStart`; flagged if the outcome is `Flagged`.
  Scrollbar counts under browser and OS furniture.
- **A flagged entry**: every one of the 15 is a marked art box, so a `Flagged`
  answer is scored as the whole image: no clip, furniture present, does
  **not** meet the bar (MC-063's rule).
- **Meets the bar**: no clip and furniture-free. The bar is **0 clips on 15
  of 15** (absolute) **and at least 14 of 15** meeting it: the brief's 9 in 10
  of 15 (13.5) rounded up, the arithmetic that gave MC-051 18 of 19 and
  MC-063 23 of 25. The user confirms 14 of 15 before AC-3 (Open question 1).

## Open questions — answered 2026-10-01

Put to the user in these words. Each has a recommended answer.

1. **Is 14 of 15 the bar?** Nine in ten of 15 is 13.5, rounded up to 14, the
   same rule that made the last two bars 18 of 19 and 23 of 25. Cutting into
   the art stays at zero, no exceptions. A screenshot the app leaves
   uncropped counts as a miss.
   - *Recommended: yes, 14 of 15.*
2. **Four of your boxes stop a little short of the bottom bar. Do you mark
   what is below them?** The 15 boxes all start right under the browser bar,
   and 11 end right on the taskbar, so those need nothing more. Four stop 1
   or 8 rows short. The strip below each is either page background, which a
   crop may keep, or a browser scrollbar or site footer, which it may not.
   Last time, four such strips turned out to be the scrollbar.
   - *Recommended: yes, on a page showing just those four, with three lines
     each (scrollbar starts, site footer starts, taskbar starts), any of
     which can be "none visible".* About 5 minutes. The page shows none of the
     app's lines and none of your boxes.
   - *Alternative:* skip it and treat the bottom of each box as the bar. A
     crop that rightly keeps page background below the art would then count as
     a failure.
3. **After the test, may I look at the screenshots that fail, one by one?**
   - *Recommended: yes, only the failures,* as last time, and only after the
     totals are written down. The totals and per-site counts are reported for
     all 15; each failing screenshot gets its cause and its rows. Those
     failures then move to the practice set, in a small follow-up story, and
     any fix is its own story. The ones that pass stay unexamined.
   - *Alternatives:* totals only (keeps all 15 unexamined, but a failure
     cannot be diagnosed), or a line for every screenshot (all 15 become
     practice).
4. **What happens if a crop cuts into the art?**
   - *Recommended: stop and ask* before anything else is acted on. That
     screenshot is named, moves to practice later, a fix is filed, and nothing
     is tested again on this batch.
5. **Score only what the app does by default?** The app adds no border
   around a crop. The tests also try a 3-pixel border, as a check.
   - *Recommended: score only the app's default (no border),* as last time.
     That is what you get when you run it.
   - *Alternative:* report both. The second number would not change the
     verdict and would double what has to be explained.
6. **If it passes, does that mean the crop is good enough?** This batch has
   no site the cropper has never seen, no screenshot that should be left
   alone and no light page, so a pass says nothing about those, and the last
   two results still stand.
   - *Recommended:* record the pass as "meets the bar on sites it knows, at
     this version", next to the last two results, and keep the goal open
     until there is evidence on a site the cropper has never seen. Your words
     were that it "should work on any reader".
   - *Alternative:* treat a pass as meeting the goal.
