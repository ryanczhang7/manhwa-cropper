## Context

**This story is parked and is not ready to start. Do not put it into RED.**

It exists because MC-019 was split by axis on 2026-09-17 (MC-019
`## Amendments`), and a split is only honest if the half that was given up is
carried somewhere rather than dropped. MC-019 now asserts the 90% bar on the
**column** axis, where the detector measures 20 of 21 inside the 11 px window.
This story owns the **row** axis, where it measures **0 of 21** and every entry
overshoots outward by 97 to 310 px.

### Why it is parked rather than startable

A story is startable when someone can name the tests before writing them. Here
the tests are obvious — they are MC-019's, with the row edges asserted — and the
*implementation* is what nobody can name. **Five** stories have now measured this
axis and all five failed (the count said "three" while the table held four; it
is corrected here rather than left to rot):

| Story | What it tried | Best result |
|---|---|---|
| MC-025 | a row locator by flatness where the gradient goes blind | moved the corpus by **zero** entries |
| MC-026 | an interior-band rule (findings 1, 1b, 5) | **5 of 21**, **8 of 21**, and 0 of 21 with 17–20 clips |
| MC-028 | panel gutters — 7 rule shapes, 264 parameterisations (`docs/wiki/panel-gutter-search.md`) | **4 of 21 with 6 clips**; cherry-picked ceiling 7 of 21 |
| MC-031 | full-width browser/OS chrome — 12 rule shapes, 1312 parameterisations (`docs/wiki/chrome-row-search.md`) | **5 of 21 with 5 clips**; the perfect chrome oracle scores **0 of 21** |
| MC-034 | the panel's own art edge — 2 families, 780 parameterisations (`docs/wiki/panel-edge-search.md`) | **8 of 21 with 9 clips**; best clip-free **0 of 21**; measured ceiling **10 of 21** |

MC-028 and MC-031 fail in complementary ways: MC-028 found *too many* candidate
rows with nothing to rank them; MC-031 found *exactly one* per edge, ranked
unambiguously, and it is the wrong row — the browser viewport rather than the
panel.

### The three facts that bound any future attempt

Measured by the orchestrator independently of both spikes' harnesses; the
numbers and commands are in MC-019 `## Amendments`.

1. **The chrome signal cannot close it, structurally.** The located chrome end
   takes **2 distinct values across the 21 images**; the marked top takes **19**.
   A signal with two values cannot predict a target with nineteen, whatever rule
   reads it. This does not depend on any threshold.
2. **The corpus offers only five clean cases.** Marked panels separated from
   their surroundings by a flat run of >= 6 rows within the 11 px window:
   **5 of 21 bounded on both sides, 7 on one side, 9 on neither.** The five are
   `2025-08-05 00_11_13.webp`, `2025-08-05 00_11_27.webp`, `Screenshot (67).png`,
   `Screenshot (2698).jpg` and `Screenshot (3538).png`. MC-028's 4 of 21 and
   MC-031's 5 of 21, reached by unrelated rule families, are both this subset.
3. **Nine entries have no flat gutter on either side of the mark.** For those the
   marked edge sits inside a content run — `2025-10-20 15_37_25.png`'s bottom
   mark at row 1302 is inside the run 1230..1309, with more content below it.
   There is no separator there to find, at any threshold.

### What would unpark it

One of these, and none of them is a tuning change:

- **A signal class outside v1** — layout or semantic segmentation of the strip
  (MC-031 section 9, Option D). Out of scope for v1 and not costed. This is the
  only route that leaves the manifest alone.
- **A product decision that the marks mean something else** — MC-031 section 9
  Option C. Under the *loose* convention (the page's vertical extent rather than
  the panel's) a rule demonstrably exists and MC-031 section 4 is it: one
  candidate per edge, ranked k = 1 and k = 2, 0 clips, one threshold separating
  99.70% of rows. The user declined to re-mark once, before that evidence
  existed. Reopening it is a product call, not an agent's.
- **A finding about the screenshots themselves.** Fact 3 may mean those nine
  screenshots cut a panel mid-way, so the mark records the visible portion rather
  than a whole panel. If so no pixel rule can ever recover those edges and the
  bar itself has to change. Cheap to settle: open the nine and look.

**Whoever picks this up starts by reading `docs/wiki/panel-gutter-search.md` and
`docs/wiki/chrome-row-search.md` in full.** Re-running a measurement either has
already taken is waste; the value of both documents is that they say what does
*not* work, with the commands that show it.

Gate that fails if this breaks: `integration`, required by this story.

## Notes

### All 21 marked entries opened and read by eye, 2026-09-17

Prompted by this story's own suggestion ("Cheap to settle: open the nine and
look"). Every marked entry was rendered as two strips - the top mark and the
bottom mark, +/- 85 rows in the marked columns, with the marked row drawn - and
looked at, with the user ruling on the marks only they can rule on.

**The "nine" does not reproduce, and the count is threshold-sensitive.** Fact 2
in `## Context` says 5 of 21 bounded on both sides, 7 on one, 9 on neither. An
independent instrument (a row is flat if fewer than 3 pixels in the marked
columns deviate more than 40 luma from the row median; a mark is bounded if >= 6
consecutive flat rows fall inside its +/- 11 px window) gives **4 / 12 / 5**. The
"both" sets agree except `2025-08-05 00_11_27.webp`, which lands at a run of 5
against a threshold of 6. Neither count is wrong; the partition is simply not
robust to the flatness definition, and no argument should rest on it.

**A sharper split does hold, and is not recorded anywhere above:** run@bottom is
**0 for 13 of 21** entries, while run@top is >= 6 for **16 of 21**. The row axis
is not uniformly hard - the deficit is overwhelmingly the *bottom* edge.

#### The four causes, replacing "there is no separator there to find"

`## Context` fact 3 says nine entries have no separator "at any threshold". That
is true of only one of the four things actually going on. In the other three a
boundary is present and plainly visible to a person, and flatness is simply the
wrong instrument for it:

1. **An overhanging speech bubble crosses the gutter.** The most common. The
   gutter is white and wide; a bubble from the panel above hangs down into it,
   or the next panel's bubble rises up, so no row in the window is flat.
   `Screenshot (2630)`, `Screenshot (1661)`, `2026-01-05 13_33_41`,
   `2025-10-14 23_30_20`, `2026-01-05 13_45_59`, `Screenshot (70)`,
   `Screenshot (2708)`.
2. **The mark is the browser viewport edge - the panel really is cut.** This is
   fact 3's hypothesis, and it is right for these: the top marks of
   `2025-08-05 00_11_13`, `2025-08-05 00_11_27`, `Screenshot (2744)`,
   `Screenshot (3187)`, `Screenshot (75)`, `Screenshot (3538)` and
   `2025-10-14 23_29_06` sit immediately under the tab bar or a browser
   notification bar, with art starting at the line. There is no panel boundary
   in the image to find. **Only this cause makes the task ill-posed.**
3. **Diagonal gutter** - the boundary is not a row at all. Two confirmed by the
   user: `Screenshot (93).jpg` (top) and `2025-10-20 15_37_25.png` (bottom).
4. **Dark art against a dark gutter** - `Screenshot (103)`, `Screenshot (2582)`:
   gutter and art are both black, so flatness cannot separate them although a
   reader can.

**What this changes for the story.** MC-028 and MC-031 both searched for the
*gutter* and tested it by flatness, which causes 1, 3 and 4 defeat by
construction while the panel's own edge stays visible. "Both pixel-level
directions are now closed" is therefore too strong: what is closed is
*flatness-tested gutter detection*. A rule family that looks for the panel edge
rather than the empty gutter has not been tried. That is not a prediction that
it works - causes 2 and 4 would still be untouched, and cause 2 alone covers
seven top edges - only a correction to the premise this story is parked on.

#### The user's rulings on the marks

Two marks were put to the user, who drew them; per this project's rule only they
can say whether a mark or the detector is wrong.

- **`Screenshot (2744).jpg`, bottom (row 1298).** The line cuts through a caption
  box ("IF DELPHINE HAD BEEN ON THE 40TH FLOOR...") that straddles it. Ruling:
  **the panel's art edge is the boundary**; the caption box is not included. The
  mark stands.
- **`2025-10-20 15_37_25.png`, bottom (row 1302).** Ruling: the art continues
  below the line because **the white gutter there is diagonal**. The mark stands.
- **Diagonal gutters carry inherent slack.** The user's rule, stated here for the
  first time: where the gutter is diagonal, a mark on the **higher edge, the
  lower edge or the middle** of the diagonal is all equally acceptable. It is
  not a hard line. This is a tolerance on the manifest and it is currently
  recorded nowhere - `fixtures/corpus/manifest.json` stores one exact rectangle
  with no note that a range was intended.

#### Two measurements that failed, recorded so nobody repeats them

- **Taskbar-relative bottom marks.** To test cause 2 numerically: find the top of
  the uniform bottom band and compare it with each bottom mark. Only **2 of 21**
  bottom marks land within 11 px of it and 7 within 30 px; the rest have 63 to
  308 px of page below them. So the strong form of cause 2 - "the bottom mark is
  just the window bottom" - is **not** supported, even though the images show it
  happening on individual files. The instrument also broke outright on the four
  dark-theme entries (`00_11_13`, `00_11_27`, `15_37_25`, `13_33_41`), where the
  page median matches the taskbar median and it reported a 1326 px taskbar.
- **Automatic diagonal detection.** Per column of the marked range, the row of
  strongest vertical gradient within +/- 40 rows, then the slope and r2 of those
  rows against x. It finds `2025-10-20 15_37_25`'s top edge clearly (-50.3 rows
  per 100 columns, r2 0.47) and everything else is noise: `Screenshot (93)`,
  which the user identified by eye, scores only 14.9 at r2 0.12, and the user's
  own diagonal on `15_37_25` is its *bottom* edge, which the instrument scores
  flat. Art texture dominates the gradient. **The diagonal-gutter entries cannot
  currently be identified automatically**, so any tagging needs a person or a
  better instrument.

#### A corpus gap this turned up

`diagonal-gutter` is one of two tags `tests/common/corpus.rs` says were defined
"so MC-019 can report on those cases separately". It is applied to **zero of the
28 entries**, and at least two entries need it. `Screenshot (93).jpg` is also
MC-019's sole column miss, at +20 on the right - and the diagonal explains it:
when the gutter runs diagonally the page's left and right edges are row-dependent,
so no single pair of columns is right for the whole page. MC-019's RED recorded
that this miss is "structural, not a threshold away" without being able to say
why. This is why.

### Parked by decision, 2026-09-17

**The user parked this story and closed EPIC-05 on 2026-09-17**, after MC-034
returned the third reasoned negative on this axis. It is not abandoned and not
deleted: it is where the row-axis debt is recorded, and EPIC-05's closing
amendment points here.

What would have to change for it to be startable, in descending order of how
likely each is to arrive:

1. **A re-score under the gutter-crossing tolerance. This is the cheapest thing
   left and it is now unblocked.** MC-034 section 7's question — may a mark sit
   below a gutter, so the rectangle spans one? — was put to the user with images
   on 2026-09-17 and **answered the same day**: on all three entries
   (`Screenshot (2708).jpg` 1305/1322, `Screenshot (3538).png` 1314/1330,
   `Screenshot (2698).jpg` 1080/1085) **either the top of the gutter or the
   recorded mark is acceptable**. The ruling is recorded on
   `docs/wiki/corpus.md`, "The gutter-crossing tolerance".

   It is neither of the two outcomes MC-034 anticipated. The families are not
   mis-specified, and the three are not mis-marked: the marks carry a **band**,
   the way the `diagonal-gutter` entries do. So **MC-034's bottom-edge numbers
   are a lower bound** — 11 of 21 reachable, and a best rule of 8 of 21 with 9
   clips, were scored against an exact mark on entries where a second answer was
   also right. How much of the deficit that recovers is **unmeasured**, and
   measuring it needs no new rule and no new signal: it is MC-034's own harness
   with the scoring predicate widened on the entries carrying a band.

   Two cautions for whoever does it. The ruling covers **three entries**, not
   the corpus; extending the band to a fourth is a person's call, exactly as a
   third `diagonal-gutter` tag is. And a widened predicate must not be allowed
   to widen silently — a band that grows until the numbers improve is not a
   tolerance, it is the oracle being edited to pass a test.

   **Measured on 2026-09-17 by MC-035 — `docs/wiki/gutter-band-rescore.md`. It
   is no longer unmeasured, and it recovers nothing that matters here:** 387 of
   8 659 clipped scorings retire and the spread family's best member gains two
   entries at the same clip count, but the best **clip-free** rule stays at
   **0 of 21** in both families, because no member of either family is clip-free
   at all — 0 of 780, before, after, and under a band twice the ruled width.
   Both cautions were turned into controls and both fired. The parking stands
   with its reason unchanged.
2. **A signal class outside v1** — colour or chroma, ruled out in MC-025's
   `## Context`; or learning-based detection, which EPIC-05 excludes by name.
3. **A product decision about what the marks mean.** MC-034 section 7 notes one
   the measurements support and does not recommend: splitting MC-019's bar by
   **edge** as it was split by axis, since the top edge is at 20 of 21 reachable
   and the bottom is not. MC-019's criteria are frozen and DONE, so that is the
   user's call and not a story's.

**Not a tuning change.** Four stories have now measured this axis and the best
clip-free result any of them reports is 1 of 21 (MC-028). Nothing in `Tuning`
moves that.
