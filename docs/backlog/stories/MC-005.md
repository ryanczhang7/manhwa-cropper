## Context

The brief's second mechanism: browser tab strips, URL bars, reader toolbars
and sidebars are not uniform, so the trim leaves them; this story removes
them. `architecture.md` (`content`, and decision 13) defines the rule: on
each side of the current rect, the outermost strip is the band between that
edge and the nearest strong line (MC-004) parallel to it; it is
**chrome-like** when its extent is at most `chrome_max_extent` of the image
dimension on that axis, its **flat fraction** (share of its pixels within
`uniform_tolerance` of the strip's median luma) is at least
`chrome_flat_fraction`, and what remains after removing it still has luma
standard deviation at or above `min_content_stddev`. Chrome-like strips are
removed and the loop repeats until no strip qualifies. A strip whose flat
fraction lies in `[chrome_flat_fraction - ambiguity_band, chrome_flat_fraction)`
is kept and marks the result ambiguous. What remains is the content box.

Decision 13 explains why this is not "the largest region": a multi-panel
page must keep all its panels. Fixtures extend the MC-003 generator with
chrome bands: a flat background with sparse 1-px "text" dashes at a chosen
flat fraction, and a strong boundary line against the art.

Gate that fails if this breaks: `unit` — `gate | unit | required` in
`.claude/harness/project.conf:157`, so it is already binding repo-wide, and
`coverage` (also required, `:158`) judges the new module too. Nothing here
needs an optional gate escalating to required, so `required_gates` stays
empty. Checked against `project.conf` in PLANNED, before the phase moved.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** - Given a `Luma` with a chrome band across the top (height 6% of `H`, flat fraction 0.90, mean luma differing from the art's first row by at least `edge_threshold`) above textured art (stddev >= 12, flat fraction <= 0.5), when `content_box(&luma, full_rect, &Tuning::default())` runs, then `rect` equals the art rows exactly (top edge at the band's last row + 1), `removed == [Side::Top]`, `ambiguous == false`.
- **AC-2** - Given the AC-1 image plus a chrome-like sidebar down the left (width 15% of `W`, flat fraction 0.90), when computed, then `rect` equals the art rect exactly and `removed` contains both `Top` and `Left` in the order they were peeled.
- **AC-3** - Given the top strip's flat fraction is 0.50 (it is art, not chrome), when computed, then nothing is removed and `rect == within`. *Controls on the number:* flat fraction 0.86 is removed; 0.84 is not removed and `ambiguous == true`; 0.79 is not removed and `ambiguous == false`.
- **AC-4** - Given a flat-fraction-0.95 top strip whose height is 31% of `H`, when computed, then it is not removed (`chrome_max_extent`); at 29% it is.
- **AC-5** - Given two stacked chrome bands at the top (tab strip, then a bookmarks bar) each bounded by its own strong line, when computed, then both are removed in two passes and `rect` starts below the second.
- **AC-6** - Given a rect with no strong lines inside it, when computed, then `rect == within` and `removed` is empty.
- **AC-7** - Given a chrome-like top strip whose removal would leave a region with stddev below `min_content_stddev` (the "art" is nearly flat), when computed, then the strip is not removed - chrome is never peeled off an image that has no content to keep.

## Model guidance

Model: default. Oracle partition:

- **Settled**: `chrome_flat_fraction = 0.85`, `chrome_max_extent = 0.30`, `ambiguity_band = 0.05`, `min_content_stddev = 12`, `uniform_tolerance = 10`, `edge_threshold = 24`. Read them out of `Tuning::default()`.
- **Mechanical**: `content_box(&Luma, Rect, &Tuning) -> ContentBox { rect: Rect, removed: Vec<Side>, ambiguous: bool }`, `Side { Top, Bottom, Left, Right }`. Pin.
- **Oracle-free**: the generator's chrome texture must hit a requested flat fraction within +/-0.01 - the generator asserts its own output and the handoff records the measured fractions for each control in AC-3.

## Out of scope

Uniform trimming inside the box afterwards and the outward margin (MC-006),
deciding a flag from `ambiguous` (MC-007), strips that do not touch an edge
(interior panel borders are never removed, by design), colour-based
chrome detection.

### The fixtures, and why they are shaped the way they are

Every scene is 100x100, so a percentage in an acceptance criterion is a pixel
count: AC-1's "6% of H" is 6 rows, AC-2's "15% of W" is 15 columns, AC-4's 31%
and 29% are 31 and 29 rows. Three ingredients:

* **`common::chrome_band(w, h, background, deviation, tolerance, flat_fraction)`**
  - the new generator. A flat background with exactly
  `round((1 - flat_fraction) * w * h)` "text" pixels on it, evenly spread by a
  Bresenham selection (so the count is exact) and alternating `+deviation` /
  `-deviation` (so the median is exactly the background at *every* flat
  fraction, including AC-3's 0.50, where a one-sided deviation would leave the
  median undefined by convention). It asserts its own output on every call:
  the share on the background, and the flat fraction that implies at the given
  tolerance. `deviation` is 20 - twice `uniform_tolerance`, so a text pixel is
  unambiguously not flat, and small enough that no band in this file contains
  a strong line of its own (measured maximum inside a band: 20.000 across
  adjacent columns, 9.333 across adjacent rows, against `edge_threshold` 24).
* **`common::soft_art`** (MC-004's texture) as the art. Measured at 100x94,
  seed 7: standard deviation **18.058** (AC-1 needs >= 12), flat fraction
  **0.4084** (AC-1 needs <= 0.5), no strong line in either direction. MC-003's
  checkerboard art would be a strong line at every row and is unusable here.
* **`common::flat_band`** (MC-003's) as AC-7's nearly flat art: a checkerboard
  of 40 and 50, standard deviation exactly **5.000**, below
  `min_content_stddev`.

Scenes are assembled by two local helpers, `stack` and `beside`.

### Three tests that pin the rule beyond what a single-index seam can

A first pass of this suite left three mutations of a reference implementation
alive, and three tests were added to kill them. They are recorded here because
each is a real hole the acceptance criteria do not close:

* **the run's outer bound.** With every seam one profile index wide,
  `Line::start` and `Line::end` are the same number and swapping them changes
  nothing. `textured_seam` puts a one-pixel textured line (130/170 alternating)
  between each band and the art, making each seam a run of **two** indices
  (measured: `(5, 6)` and `(92, 93)`), so `start` at the top and `end` at the
  bottom are distinguishable. The seam line is textured rather than flat on
  purpose: a flat seam line would itself be a one-pixel chrome strip on the
  next pass. Its flat fraction measures 0.5000, well below the ambiguity band.
* **the median.** With symmetric deviations the band's mean equals its median,
  so "about the median" and "about the mean" agree. `dark_dashed_band` makes
  10% of the columns dark (60 against a background of 200), in the same columns
  on every row - which keeps the adjacent-row difference at 0 and the whole
  image's adjacent-column maximum at 15.410, both under 24. Median **200**,
  mean **186**: flat fraction 0.9000 about the median and 0.0000 about the mean.
* **one strip per side per pass.** AC-5's "two passes" is not observable
  through the API - a greedy drain of the top side produces the same
  `[Top, Top]` and the same rect. Adding a sidebar makes it observable: one
  strip per side per pass gives `[Top, Left, Top]`, a greedy drain gives
  `[Top, Top, Left]`.

### One measurement that did not become an assertion

`a_top_strip_of_exactly_the_chrome_max_extent_is_peeled` does not pin `<=`
over `<` under every formulation. `0.30f32` is 0.30000001192, so
`30.0 <= 0.30f32 * 100.0` and `30.0 < 0.30f32 * 100.0` are **both** true and
the multiplication form is insensitive to the operator. Written as a division -
`extent / dimension <= t.chrome_max_extent` - the quotient is bit-for-bit
`0.30f32` and the test does discriminate: measured, the mutation from `<=` to
`<` fails exactly that test. The test earns its place either way (it pins that
a 30-row strip of a 100-row image is chrome, which is the behaviour), but the
handoff records the limit honestly rather than claiming a boundary the
arithmetic does not have.

**The rule the tests pin, stated once.** On each side of the current rect the
outermost strip runs from that edge to the nearest strong line parallel to it,
taking the run's **outer** bound on the side nearest the edge (`Line::start`
for Top and Left, `Line::end` for Bottom and Right); a Top strip whose line is
at profile index `i` is `i + 1` lines deep, so the new top edge is the band's
last row **plus one**. The strip is chrome-like when all three hold: extent
`<= t.chrome_max_extent * ` **the image's** dimension on that axis; flat
fraction `>= t.chrome_flat_fraction`, where the flat fraction is the share of
the strip's pixels within `t.uniform_tolerance` (inclusive) of the strip's
**median** luma; and the region left after removing it has standard deviation
`>= t.min_content_stddev`. Chrome-like strips are removed, one per side per
pass, sides visited `Top, Bottom, Left, Right`, repeating until a whole pass
removes nothing. A strip that passes the extent and stddev conditions and
misses on flatness alone, by less than `t.ambiguity_band`, is kept and sets
`ambiguous` - the qualified reading settled in `## Notes`, and two tests assert
that a strip failing extent or stddev does **not** set it however flat it is.

**Do the threshold arithmetic in `f32`.** Three tests sit exactly on a
boundary. `510f32 / 600f32` is bit-for-bit `0.85f32`, so `>=` holds; the same
quotient computed in `f64` is one ulp *below* `f64::from(0.85f32)` and
`a_top_strip_exactly_at_the_flat_fraction_threshold_is_peeled` fails.
`0.85f32 - 0.05f32` is bit-for-bit `0.8f32`, and the same detour through `f64`
breaks `a_top_strip_exactly_at_the_foot_of_the_ambiguity_band_is_ambiguous`.
The `Tuning` fields are `f32`; keep them that way.

**Deliberately not constrained, so it stays your choice**: where in
`crates/core/src/` the module lives, as long as those three paths resolve;
whether `ContentBox` or `Side` gain further derives (`Clone`, `PartialEq`) or
methods; whether the standard deviation is a sample or a population one;
whether the four sides share one helper or have four; how the strip's median
is found; whether `content_box` is `#[must_use]`; and whether you add in-module
`#[cfg(test)]` tests, which are `src/` files and therefore yours.

**The `Side` decision.** `trim.rs` already has a **private** `enum Side` with
the same four variants and a `const SIDES: [Side; 4]` in the same
`Top, Bottom, Left, Right` order. The recommendation is to **move it into
`content` as the public type and have `trim` use it** (`use crate::content::Side;`),
deleting the private duplicate: two identical four-variant enums in one crate
is duplication a reviewer will ask about, and `trim`'s visiting order is
already the order `content` needs. The tests do **not** discriminate this -
`trim`'s enum is private and unobservable - so defining a separate public
`Side` in `content` and leaving `trim` alone also passes. Either way, do not
change `trim`'s behaviour: `cargo test -p cropper-core --test trim` must still
report 19 passed.

### Notes for the implementer

* **No test passed on arrival.** The module does not exist, so nothing in the
  file could run; there is no regression guard here needing a probe.
* **`common/mod.rs` is compiled separately into each test binary**, so the new
  `chrome_band` is dead code in the `trim` and `edges` targets. MC-004's
  module-level `#![allow(dead_code)]` already covers that; do not remove it.
* `edges::{row_profile, col_profile, strong_lines}` are used by the tests
  themselves to check their fixtures, and by the reference to find the strips.
  Reuse them - nothing here needs a second edge detector.
* AC-5's "two passes" is **not observable through the API** on its own scene:
  a greedy drain of the top side produces the same `[Top, Top]` and the same
  rect. `each_pass_takes_at_most_one_strip_from_each_side` is what makes the
  Notes' rule testable, and it is the test the (l) mutation above trips.
* Nothing in the story constrains what happens when `within` is empty or
  extends past the image, and nothing here asserts it. The reference guarded
  the standard deviation against a zero-area remainder before computing it;
  that is a sensible precaution rather than a requirement.
* One harness bug found and **not** fixed here, because it is out of a Test
  Developer's remit in RED: `.claude/hooks/phase-guard.sh` classifies the
  token `2>/dev/null` in a shell command as a `source` path and refuses the
  command. It should treat a file-descriptor redirect to `/dev/null` as
  writing nothing. Worth a line in `.claude/tests/phase-guard.test.sh` when
  somebody owns it.

from 47 tests to 3 and the gate would still have passed.

      -> .claude/state/gate-logs/unit.log
    1 required gate(s) failed.

## Notes

### PLANNED, 2026-09-10: one uncovered reading, decided here rather than in code

`architecture.md` states the ambiguity rule without qualification — "a strip
whose flat fraction falls within `ambiguity_band` below the threshold is left
in place and marks the result *ambiguous*". Read literally, a strip occupying
half the image, or one whose removal would leave nothing, would still flag
`ambiguous` on flat fraction alone. No acceptance criterion discriminates the
two readings: AC-3's controls all sit inside a 6%-tall strip that passes the
extent and stddev conditions, and AC-4 and AC-7 both use flat fractions at or
above 0.85, outside the band either way.

**Decided: ambiguity is qualified by the other two conditions.** A strip marks
the result ambiguous only when its extent is within `chrome_max_extent` and
what would remain still has stddev at or above `min_content_stddev` — i.e.
only a strip that is *otherwise* a chrome candidate and misses on flatness
alone. `FlagReason::Ambiguous` is documented in `architecture.md` as "an edge
strip was **nearly chrome-like**", and a strip failing the extent test is not
nearly chrome-like; the literal reading would flag ordinary art. This is an
interpretation of an uncovered case, not an amendment: no acceptance criterion
changes, and none is contradicted. Tests may pin it as an extra case beyond
the criteria, or leave it unasserted; they must not assert the other reading.

### Side visiting order

`removed` records the order strips were peeled (AC-2, AC-5). The order is
`Top, Bottom, Left, Right` per pass, matching `trim::SIDES`, and the loop
repeats until a whole pass removes nothing. AC-2 expects `[Top, Left]`, which
that order produces.
