## Context

MC-032 is parked on a premise that MC-032's own survey corrects, and this spike
exists to test the correction before anybody acts on it either way.

EPIC-05's amendment of 2026-09-17 reads MC-028 and MC-031 as closing "both
pixel-level directions" on the row axis. Both spikes searched for the **gutter**
- the empty space between two panels - and both tested a candidate row by
**flatness**. MC-032's `## Notes` ("All 21 marked entries opened and read by eye,
2026-09-17") partitions the 21 marked entries into four causes of a missing flat
gutter, and **three of the four defeat a flatness test by construction while the
panel's own edge stays plainly visible to a human reader**: an overhanging speech
bubble crossing the gutter, a diagonal gutter, and dark art against a dark
gutter. What MC-028 and MC-031 closed is *flatness-tested gutter detection*, not
row-edge detection in general.

MC-033 then made that distinction the corpus's official rule
(`docs/wiki/corpus.md`, "The marking rule", confirmed by the user 2026-09-17):

> The expected rectangle is the tightest axis-aligned rectangle containing all
> of **one page's own artwork**. **A panel's own overhang extends its rectangle;
> a neighbouring panel's overhang, or a caption box sitting in the gutter, does
> not.**

So the boundary a detector has to find is, by the corpus's own definition, the
**panel's art edge**. No story has ever searched for it directly. That is the
whole of this spike's question, and it is a question rather than a plan because
the prior below says the answer is probably no.

### The honest prior: what a panel-edge rule cannot reach

MC-032's four causes, with the entries it names. This is the evidence base and
it is **settled** - it came from opening all 21 entries with the user and is not
to be re-derived from an instrument (see "Two instruments that already failed"
below).

|---|---|---|---|

| 3 | diagonal gutter - the boundary is not a row at all | `Screenshot (93)` (top), `2025-10-20 15_37_25` (bottom) - 2, tagged `diagonal-gutter` by MC-033 and carrying an explicit tolerance | maybe, within the band `corpus.md` records |
| 4 | dark art against a dark gutter | `Screenshot (103)`, `Screenshot (2582)` - 2 | maybe - a gradient or edge rule rather than a flatness rule |

**MC-019's bar is 20 of 21 with zero clips. Cause 2 alone puts a hard ceiling
well below it**, and computing that ceiling is AC-1 - it comes first, before any
rule is scored, because a rule that scores 9 of 21 against a ceiling of 12 is a
different finding from one that scores 9 of 21 against a ceiling of 21.

Two things make the ceiling real work rather than transcription:

- **MC-032's cause lists are per-entry, not per-edge**, except cause 2 (stated
  as top edges) and cause 3 (stated per edge). MC-019 requires *both* edges of
  an entry inside the window, so each of the 42 edges has to be assigned before
  the 21 entries can be counted. MC-033 supplies two edge-level rulings the
  table above does not: `Screenshot (2744)` **bottom** at row 1298 (a caption
  box straddles the mark, and is outside the rectangle) and `Screenshot (2630)`
  **top** at row 224 (the panel above's bubble hangs across, and is outside).
- **Cause 2 is the one assignment that can be argued**, and it moves the ceiling
  by up to seven entries. MC-032 says there is no panel boundary to find; but on
  those entries the art starts at the cut line, so a rule that places the edge at
  "the outermost row of the panel's own art" lands on the mark by construction,
  and MC-031's anchored family did place `00_11_13` (+2, +7) and `00_11_27`
  (+6, +10). Whether that is a panel-edge rule succeeding or a coincidence of a
  cut is a judgement, so AC-1 requires the ceiling **both ways** with the
  judgement defended rather than assumed.

### The closest prior art is MC-031's `anchored` family, and it is already an art scan

**This is the trap this story is most likely to fall into, so it is stated
before the criteria rather than discovered in the middle.** "No story has
searched for the panel's art edge" is true only in a narrow sense.
`chrome-row-search.md`'s `anchored` family is defined as *chrome anchor, then
an art scan*: row 1 of its table reads "anchored, chrome th 1.00 / k 64, **art
`flat < 0.90`, run 8**, backoff 0", and MC-031 ran **672 parameterisations** of
it. Its best member is **5 of 21 with 5 clips**, and MC-031 section 7 describes
its own failures as "the two shapes **an art scan** cannot see": a mark sitting
inside a gutter the scan runs straight through (`23_30_20` −15, `15_37_25` −49,
`13_45_59` −1) and a panel whose far end is beyond a second gutter the scan
stops at (`13_49_39` bottom −38, `3538` bottom −98).

So a rule that scans outward for the first run of art has been tried, and it
scores 5 of 21. **A family that cannot say how it differs from `anchored` is
`anchored`, and re-deriving 5 of 21 is not a finding.** The distinction that is
genuinely open, and the one this spike exists to test:

- `anchored` is **keyed to chrome** — it starts from the browser's own
  full-width UI and scans inward. MC-031 section 6 notes the point at which "an
  art scan anchored to chrome is no longer keyed to chrome", and every one of
  its four ranked rules **fires control (c2)** by skipping the chrome block
  entirely. A rule keyed to the **panel** — its own extent, its own interior
  structure — is a different object, and is untested.
- `anchored`'s five clips are all cases where the scan's *stopping rule* is
  wrong, not where the edge is invisible. Three are marks inside a gutter the
  scan crosses; two are panels whose far end lies past a second gutter. Both are
  about deciding **which** art edge is the page's, which is exactly what
  MC-033's marking rule now answers ("a panel's own overhang extends its
  rectangle; a neighbouring panel's overhang does not") and what `anchored`,
  written before that rule existed, had no way to encode.

`anchored`'s **5 of 21 with 5 clips is therefore this story's nearest
baseline**, closer than MC-026's 8 of 21, and AC-2 and AC-4 are both scored
against it.

### The sharper split, and what it means for which edge to attack

Also from MC-032's survey, and recorded nowhere else: run@bottom is **0 for 13
of 21** entries while run@top is >= 6 for **16 of 21**. The row axis is not
uniformly hard - the deficit is overwhelmingly the **bottom** edge. MC-028's
per-edge best was top 13 of 21, bottom 7 of 21; MC-031's was top 13, bottom 8.

### Two instruments that already failed - do not repeat them

Both are in MC-032's `## Notes` under "Two measurements that failed, recorded so
nobody repeats them", and both are in `## Out of scope`:

- **Taskbar-relative bottom marks**, the attempt to test cause 2 numerically:
  only **2 of 21** bottom marks land within 11 px of the top of the uniform
  bottom band, 7 within 30 px, and the instrument **broke outright** on the four
  dark-theme entries (`00_11_13`, `00_11_27`, `15_37_25`, `13_33_41`), reporting
  a 1326 px taskbar. The strong form of cause 2 is not numerically supported, so
  **the ceiling is derived from the per-entry eye survey, which is settled and
  named** - not from an instrument.
- **Automatic diagonal detection**: it finds one edge and calls the user's own
  diagonal flat. `corpus.md` is explicit - "Diagonal entries cannot currently be
  found automatically ... Do not re-run it."

### The bar, unchanged, and what is not negotiable

MC-019's window: a produced rect lies inside the expected rect expanded by
`margin_px + 8` px (11 px on this tree) and **contains it entirely** - a clip
ranks above every other defect (MC-005 decision 13). The user fixed the 11 px
tolerance and the manifest on 2026-09-16 and re-confirmed the split on
2026-09-17 (MC-019 `## Amendments`). Neither moves here, and this story does not
re-open them.

The baselines every rule is scored against: **5 of 21** (MC-026 finding 1),
**8 of 21** (MC-026 finding 1b), **0 of 21 with 17-20 clips** (finding 5),
**4 of 21 with 6 clips** (MC-028, ceiling 7 of 21) and **5 of 21 with 5 clips**
(MC-031, best hybrid 6 of 21, perfect chrome oracle 0 of 21).

## Acceptance criteria

The deliverable is one document, `docs/wiki/panel-edge-search.md`.

- **AC-1** — *The reachability ceiling is computed from the named per-entry causes, and stated before any rule is scored.* Given the document, when its first evidence section is read, then it carries a table of all **21 marked entries and both of their edges (42 edges)**, each edge assigned one of MC-032's four causes or "clean", each assignment sourced by name to MC-032's `## Notes` survey or to `docs/wiki/corpus.md`; and from that table it states **how many of the 21 entries a panel-edge rule could place on both edges in principle** - the ceiling - with the arithmetic shown. The causes are **read out of the eye survey, not re-derived from an instrument**: MC-032 records that the taskbar-relative measurement intended to settle cause 2 numerically supports only 2 of 21 and breaks on the four dark-theme entries. Because the assignment of cause 2's seven top edges is a judgement that moves the answer by up to seven entries, the ceiling is reported **both ways** - those edges counted unreachable (MC-032's own reading) and counted reachable (the art starts at the cut line, so an art-edge rule lands on the mark) - and the document says which it uses and why. *Control on the number:* a ceiling of 21 of 21 is the vacuous answer and is rejected; at minimum the entries excluded must be named individually, and if no entry is excluded the document must say what it believes MC-032's cause 2 got wrong, with the images.
- **AC-2** — *At least one genuinely panel-edge-shaped rule family is tried and scored on the corpus with MC-019's own scoring.* Given the document, when its scoring section is read, then it reports, for every rule it scores, **both** of MC-019's numbers - how many of the 21 entries land inside the expected rect expanded by `margin_px + 8` px, **and** how many are clipped - computed over the **final, margin-expanded, clamped** rect as MC-028 section 5b and MC-031 section 8 do, with a **per-entry table** so the number is comparable with MC-028's 4 of 21 and MC-026's 5 and 8 of 21. At least one family scored is **panel-edge shaped and shown to be**: it places its edge at the outermost row of the **panel's own art** rather than at a flat run, and the document demonstrates on at least one named **cause-1** entry that it places an edge where a flatness test cannot - otherwise it is MC-028's family under a new name and must be reported as such. **It must additionally clear MC-031's `anchored` family**, which is itself an art scan at 5 of 21 with 5 clips over 672 parameterisations (see `## Context`): the document states in one sentence how the family differs **structurally** - not keyed to chrome, or a different stopping rule for which art edge is the page's - and scores it against `anchored`'s 5 of 21 / 5 clips as the **nearest** baseline. A family that cannot state the difference, or that scores at or below 5 of 21 with no fewer clips, is reported as a reproduction of `anchored` rather than as a new result, and that is a legitimate finding to report - it closes the direction. The two `diagonal-gutter` entries are scored **exactly as MC-019 scores them**; `corpus.md`'s band tolerance may be reported alongside but must never be folded into the headline number.
- **AC-3** — *The negative controls are named, and each is shown to fire.* Given any rule the document scores, when its section is read, then it names the inputs the rule must **not** fire on, what it scores on them, and shows each control firing for at least one parameterisation rather than passing vacuously: at minimum (a) **a neighbour's overhang** - a synthetic page where the panel above's speech bubble, or a caption box, hangs into the gutter below the marked edge, which `corpus.md`'s marking rule puts **outside** the rectangle; a rule that takes the outermost non-flat thing will over-reach and must be shown doing so; (b) **the seven `"expect": "flag"` corpus entries**, whose no-regression baseline is **1 of 7 already cropped** (`2025-02-27 22_46_15.png`); and (c) **a panel containing a flat full-width row**, MC-026 finding 5's defect, which MC-028 and MC-031 both ran. A rule with no control is reported as having none.
- **AC-4** — *The verdict is stated out loud, against the ceiling and against the baselines, whichever way it falls.* Given the investigation has ended, when the document's first section is read, then it states one of exactly two verdicts: **a recommended rule**, with its score and clip count under MC-019's window; or **a reasoned negative** - no rule tried reaches the bar - with the families tried and why each failed. Either verdict is stated against **both** yardsticks explicitly: against AC-1's ceiling, and against the five baselines in `## Context` - in particular **"beats 8 of 21" or "does not beat 8 of 21"**, in those words. **A reasoned negative is a valid and expected outcome**, as it was for MC-028 and MC-031; the value of this story is highest when it returns "no" early, and a score reported without AC-1's ceiling is not an answer.
- **AC-5** — *Every number is reproducible.* Given the document, when a number in it is questioned, then the document names the command that produced it and the location of the harness, as MC-025's, MC-026's, MC-028's and MC-031's `## Notes` do. No number appears without one. Numbers read out of MC-032, MC-028, MC-031 or `corpus.md` cite the section they come from instead, and are not re-measured.
- **AC-6** — *MC-032 gets a recommendation, with the reason.* Given the document ends, when its last section is read, then it recommends exactly one of: **unpark MC-032**, **keep it parked**, or **amend its criteria** - with the reason, and with what changes in MC-032's `## Context` as a result, since that `## Context` currently asserts that both pixel-level directions are closed. If the recommendation is to unpark, it states the shape of the implementation story: which module, which `Tuning` field if any, the required gate that would fail if it broke, and whether it fits one RED -> GREEN cycle. If it is to keep it parked or to amend, it states what evidence the user would need to decide, in the form MC-028 section 9 and MC-031 section 9 use - images and a question, not another spike.

## Model guidance

Model: default. Oracle partition:

- **Settled - read out, never re-derived**: MC-032's four causes and the entries
  they name; the two user rulings and the diagonal tolerance in
  `docs/wiki/corpus.md`; MC-019's 11 px window, its zero-clip rule and the
  manifest, all fixed by the user's decisions of 2026-09-16 and 2026-09-17;
  MC-005 decision 13; MC-027's columns, taken as they are; the five baselines.
  These came from a person looking at images, and MC-032 records that **no
  instrument agrees with the eye here** - an agent that "checks" them against a
  gradient will get a different answer and the different answer will be wrong.
- **Oracle-free, and the whole of the work**: every rule proposed, every
  threshold in it, and the metric each is scored by. The corpus is the oracle
  and a clip is the loss function. AC-3's control (a) is the one that has to be
  invented from scratch, because no prior spike needed it: MC-028's and MC-031's
  controls both test *under*-reaching, and a panel-edge rule's characteristic
  failure is **over**-reaching onto a neighbour's overhang, which the marking
  rule puts outside the rectangle. It must be shown firing.
- **Mechanical**: AC-2's reporting shape, AC-5, AC-6.

**Success condition, which can come out either way.** This spike succeeds if a
reader can say which is true, with numbers: *a panel-edge rule exists that
reaches MC-019's window on at least 20 of 21 with zero clips*; or *the ceiling
is N of 21 and the best rule tried is M of 21 with K clips, and here is whether
M beats 8 of 21 and why each family fails*. It fails only if it returns neither,
or if it returns M without N.

**Model-choice success condition, which can also come out either way.** The
verdict recorded so far in this repository (`story-authoring`, "Brief RED by
oracle") points at the **brief** rather than the model, so this story stays on
the default and spends its budget on the partition above. The condition that
would overturn that: if the investigation returns a score without the ceiling,
or re-derives a settled cause from an instrument, or reports a rule as
panel-edge shaped without AC-2's cause-1 demonstration, then the failure is one
of judgement under a partitioned brief and a stronger model is worth trying on
the next spike. If it returns the ceiling first and the negative controls fire,
the default was enough and the verdict is recorded here saying so. **Record the
verdict in `## Notes` when the investigation ends** - a model choice with no
recorded verdict is folklore.

**Timebox.** If the ceiling comes out below 20 of 21 and two rule families have
been scored without beating 8 of 21 - or without clearing `anchored`'s 5 of 21
with 5 clips, whichever the families are closer to - that is the reasoned
negative; write it and stop. As with MC-028 and MC-031, returning "no" early is the valuable outcome.

- **Any learning- or model-based detection**, which EPIC-05's "Deliberately not

### Model-choice verdict

`## Model guidance` asked for one and set conditions that could come out either
way. **The default was enough**, and the verdict is recorded here rather than
left as folklore.

The overturning conditions were: a score returned without the ceiling; a settled
cause re-derived from an instrument; or a family reported as panel-edge shaped
without AC-2's cause-1 demonstration. None happened. The ceiling came first
(section 2) and is what the verdict rests on; the settled causes were read out
and the one instrument that could have re-derived them was abandoned when it
failed to reproduce (2d); and the coverage family's shape is demonstrated by
control (a), where it over-reaches on 8.3 % of its parameterisations against the
spread family's 25 %.

What the brief did carry, and is worth repeating for the next spike, was the
`anchored` warning in `## Context`: without it the obvious first family - scan
outward for art - would have been reported as a new result at 6 of 21 rather
than measured against the 5 of 21 it barely improves on.
