## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** - *Zero clips.* Given every corpus entry with an expected rect, when processed, then for each entry whose outcome is `Cropped { rect, .. }`, `rect` contains the expected rect entirely (`rect.x <= e.x`, `rect.y <= e.y`, `rect.x + rect.w >= e.x + e.w`, `rect.y + rect.h >= e.y + e.h`). One violation fails the test and names the file and the offending side. *Control on the metric:* with every expected rect widened by 1 px on the top before comparison, the check must fail on at least one entry (recorded in the handoff; if it does not, the detector's margin is hiding the comparison and the control must move to 4 px).
- **AC-2** - *Nine in ten.* Given every corpus entry, when processed, then at least 90% are *right*: an entry with an expected rect is right when the outcome is `Cropped` and `rect` lies inside the expected rect expanded by `margin_px + 8` px on every side (AC-1 having held); an entry with `"expect": "flag"` is right when the outcome is `Flagged`. A flagged entry that expected a rect is a miss, and a cropped entry that expected a flag is a miss. The test prints a per-file table (file, expected, got, right/miss, reason).
- **AC-3** - Given the run, when it completes, then `failed() == 0` - every corpus file is readable and writable.
- **AC-4** - Given the constants in `Tuning::default()` changed in this story, when the story ends, then `## Notes` carries the table of old and new values with AC-1/AC-2 figures before and after, and `docs/wiki/architecture.md`'s tuning table is updated to match.

## Model guidance

Model: default. Oracle partition:

- **Settled**: 90%, `margin_px + 8`, the containment predicate. Read out.
- **Mechanical**: AC-3, AC-4.
- **Oracle-free**: nothing - the corpus is the oracle. But the corpus can be wrong: a "clip" on one file may be a mis-marked rect. The Feature Developer reports that as a finding with the file name; the orchestrator opens the image and decides, with the user, whether the manifest or the detector is wrong, and records which in `## Notes`. Never edit the manifest to make the test pass without that record.

## Out of scope

Per-reader special cases, any detection technique not already in
`cropper-core`, tuning beyond `Tuning::default()`'s existing fields, adding
corpus files (MC-018, or a follow-up chore).

### Orchestrator's independent reproduction of MC-028's escalation (PLANNED)

MC-028 came back a reasoned negative and its section 9 names **the manifest**
as the number that must move. That is a claim that the contract is wrong, so it
was reproduced here before being accepted - fresh code, different instrument,
different columns, sharing nothing with MC-025/026/028's harnesses. Harness:
`<session scratchpad>/rowprobe` (no cropper dependency; its own integer BT.601,
median, flat fraction and band walk) and `<session scratchpad>/shipped` (the
public `process_file` only).

**1. What reproduces exactly.** `cargo run --bin shipped`, the shipped detector
at `Tuning::default()` over all 28 entries:

```
clips (AC-1 violations): 0
  entries inside the mark expanded by  11 px:  0 / 21
  entries inside the mark expanded by 300 px: 20 / 21
worst COL overshoot per entry, sorted: [4,5,5,6,6,6,6,7,7,8,8,9,9,10,10,10,10,10,10,10,20]
worst ROW overshoot per entry, sorted: [97,103,105,110,134,134,151,155,156,179,183,
                                        191,197,203,232,275,282,283,285,294,310]
6 of 7 flag entries Flagged; `2025-02-27 22_46_15.png` Cropped (the known baseline)
```

MC-028 section 4 and section 9's slack table are confirmed: columns are solved
(20 of 21 within 10 px, the one outlier `Screenshot (93).jpg` at +20), the row
axis carries the entire deficit, and nothing clips.

**2. What does NOT reproduce: the evidence for a broken manifest.** MC-028's
decisive pair is `2025-10-14 23_30_20.png` and `2025-10-20 15_37_25.png`, "the
same structure ... art much lower ... marked at 171 and 228. A 57 px
disagreement between two marks of one pixel feature." The marks and the shared
chrome edge reproduce - both files' chrome ends at row 166 - but the premise
does not. `cargo run --bin rowprobe`, row medians over the marked columns:

```
-- 2025-10-14 23_30_20.png   rows 140..166 tone ~59 FLAT | 167..168 tone ~255 FLAT
                             rows 169..173 tone ~255 textured   <- art starts at 169
-- 2025-10-20 15_37_25.png   rows 140..166 tone ~59 FLAT | 167..239 tone ~255 FLAT
                             rows 240..242 tone ~255 textured   <- art starts at 240
```

The two files are **not** structurally identical below row 167: one has art two
rows after the chrome, the other has 73 rows of white first. Both marks sit in
the gap between the art above and the art below. The 57 px is the difference
between two pages, not between two conventions, and the images confirm it -
`23_30_20` is marked exactly on the speech bubble that starts the panel;
`15_37_25` is marked between a bubble tail from the panel above and the panel
below.

**Why MC-028's instrument saw it differently.** Its "homogeneous band" is a
per-row flat fraction >= 0.90, and a speech-bubble tail 3 px wide across 500
columns moves that by 0.006. The three deepest cases it reports are all this:
`Screenshot (103).jpg` is marked exactly at a bubble tail's tip (50 rows "inside
a homogeneous band" that contains the tail), and `2026-01-05 13_49_39.png` and
`Screenshot (2582).jpg` are marked 4 and 3 rows below the art above them, with
the band's far end counted as the depth.

**3. The opposite claim fails its own control, so the manifest is not vindicated
either.** An art-sensitive instrument (>= 3 pixels deviating from the row median
by > 40) puts all 42 marked edges within 10 px of an art boundary, 33 exactly on
one - but `cargo run --bin control` scores every other row in the same span the
same way:

```
MARKED edges     n=42     within 0 px: 33/42 = 78.6%   within 11 px: 42/42 = 100.0%
EVERY other row  n=22871  within 0 px: 22745/22871 = 99.4%  within 11 px: 99.9%
```

Ordinary rows beat the marks. "Sits at an art boundary" is **vacuous**, and this
statistic is withdrawn. It does independently corroborate MC-028 section 5f's
ordinal finding - candidates are dense and nothing in a row profile ranks them -
which remains the live obstacle.

**4. New, and it decides the tolerance question: the overshoot is not benign.**
MC-028 section 9 calls the failure mode benign because it never clips, leaving
only the tolerance violated. The produced crops say otherwise. `Screenshot
(2698).jpg` (+306/+310, the worst) carries a strip of the **browser URL bar**
(`eed-manhwa/chapter-406/`) at the top and the next panel's speech bubble at the
bottom. `2025-08-05 00_11_13.webp` (+99/+110, the **best** of the 21) carries
**two rows of browser tabs** at the top and the **Windows taskbar** at the
bottom. Both written by `cargo run --bin shipped` and viewed.

So the overshoot runs past the page into browser and OS chrome on every entry,
and raising the slack to the 294 px MC-028 computes would not make the outputs
acceptable - it would make AC-2 stop measuring the defect while all 21 outputs
still need a manual fix. **The 11 px tolerance must not move.**

**5. One direction MC-028 reports as tested is actually untested.** Section 5d
scores "the pixels beside the page column" at 0 of 21 and explains it by the
column being narrower than the toolbars. But it profiled those pixels *inside
the pipeline rect*, and this tree's pipeline rect is 2 to 10 px wider than the
column - there were almost no pixels beside it to read. Over the **full image
width** the margins do carry the signal (`cargo run --bin chrome`): the bottom
scan lands on the taskbar at 1399 or 1439 repeatedly and 2 of 21 fall within
11 px of the mark. That is **not a rule** - the naive top scan is 0 of 21 - and
designing one is not the orchestrator's remit. It is recorded only because
full-width chrome peeling on the row axis is a different question from the
panel-gutter search MC-028 scoped itself to, and it has not yet been asked.

**Conclusion put to the user.** None of MC-019's three numbers has been shown to
need moving: the tolerance must not (4), the manifest's inconsistency is not
established (2, 3), and the 90% is reachable if the rows are (1). What is
established is that the row axis is unsolved and that every output currently
contains browser or OS chrome.
