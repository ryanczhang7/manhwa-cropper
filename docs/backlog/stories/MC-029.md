## Context

`docs/wiki/audits/app-window-2026-09-13.md`, evidence **E-4**:
`rounded_rect_outline` contributes **thirty** surviving mutants on its own,
including

```
crates/app/src/gui.rs:638:5: replace rounded_rect_outline -> Vec<Pos2> with vec![]
crates/app/src/gui.rs:662:32: replace + with - in rounded_rect_outline
```

The first removes the drop zone's border **entirely** and no test notices. Four
more in `drop_zone` are the inset that keeps the boundary from being half
clipped by the rect it is drawn on:

```
crates/app/src/gui.rs:376:46: replace / with % in drop_zone   (rect.shrink(stroke.width / 2.0))
crates/app/src/gui.rs:376:46: replace / with * in drop_zone
crates/app/src/gui.rs:377:44: replace - with + in drop_zone   (RADIUS_ZONE - stroke.width / 2.0)
crates/app/src/gui.rs:377:59: replace / with * in drop_zone
```

This story was split out of MC-021 on 2026-09-15, when that story was re-planned
around the frame's paint list instead of a renderer. Read **MC-021's `## Context`
first**: it establishes the instrument, and this story inherits it rather than
re-deriving it.

**The border is values, not pixels.** `drop_zone` builds the outline as a
polyline and hands it to `egui::Shape::dashed_line`, which at the pinned
`epaint-0.36.2` emits one `Shape::LineSegment { points: [Pos2; 2], stroke }` per
dash (`src/shapes/shape.rs`, `dashes_from_line`). So a test can read the dashes
back out of `Harness::output().shapes` as coordinates — where the border is,
whether it is closed, whether its corners are round, and whether it exists at
all. Confirm that the segments actually arrive in this window's frame before
sizing; MC-021 will have answered the same question for rects.

**Thirty survivors is not thirty tests, and the audit says so.** E-4 checked
exactly one of them for equivalence and found it *is* equivalent at the only
call site that exists: `gui.rs:642:68 replace / with *` widens
`radius.clamp(0.0, min(w, h) / 2.0)` to `min(w, h) * 2.0`, and since the zone is
120 px tall and the radius asked for is 7.5, the clamp is inactive either way
and the painted output is byte-identical. **No test can kill that one**, and a
story that adds tests until a number reaches zero will add tests that assert
nothing. The other twenty-nine have never been worked through; doing that is
part of this story's RED (see `## Model guidance`).

**Line numbers drift; identify every mutation by what it does.** At this
story's base commit `fn rounded_rect_outline` is `gui.rs:632`, the clamp is
`gui.rs:636`, and `drop_zone`'s inset is still `gui.rs:376`–`377`. Confirm at
your own base commit.

The required gate that would fail if this story's artifact broke is **`unit`**
(`cargo test --workspace`).

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** — Given the window is idle, when the drop zone is painted, then its
  boundary is painted as a series of separate dashes — more than one segment,
  each stroked 1 px in `border-strong` (`tokens.md`, "Radii, borders,
  elevation"). *This is the criterion `replace rounded_rect_outline with vec![]`
  has to fail: with no outline there are no segments at all.*

- **AC-2** — Given the same frame, when the dashes' extent is compared with the
  drop zone's own rect, then the boundary sits inside it by half the stroke
  width on every side, within a tolerance no larger than half a point.
  *This is the inset at `gui.rs:376`. A mutation that insets by the wrong
  amount, or not at all, moves every dash by a measurable distance.*

- **AC-3** — Given the same frame, when every dash endpoint is tested against
  the rounded rectangle it is meant to lie on — the inset rect at radius
  `radius-zone` less half the stroke width — then every one lies on it within
  the tolerance the polyline's own corner approximation needs, and **no point
  lies in the square corner that a radius of zero would produce**.
  *Control: the second clause is what makes this about round corners rather
  than about any closed path. State the tolerance and where it comes from —
  six segments per quarter circle at radius ~7 — and show that the square-corner
  point it must reject is far outside it.*

- **AC-4** — Given files are hovering over the window, when the drop zone is
  painted, then its boundary is a single stroked rect and not a series of
  dashes (`components.md`: the hover state is solid, `primary`, 2 px).
  *Control for AC-1: a painter that dashed unconditionally would satisfy AC-1,
  AC-2 and AC-3 and be wrong.*

- **AC-5** — Given the idle frame, when the painted dashes are measured, then
  the total painted length is 6/10 of the boundary's length, within 10 %, and
  the gap between consecutive dashes is 4 points within the same tolerance
  (`tokens.md`: 6 px dash, 4 px gap).
  *Control: a solid outline scores 10/10 and must fail; so must a dash pattern
  with the two constants swapped, which scores 4/10. Both are well outside the
  band, and the story records the three measured numbers.*

- **AC-6** — Given the shipped painter mutated so that `rounded_rect_outline`
  returns `vec![]` (today `gui.rs:632`), when the new tests run, then at least
  one fails. And given it mutated so that the corner angles step by `-` instead
  of `+` (today `gui.rs:656`), when they run, then at least one fails.
  *Two named survivors as this story's negative controls: one removes the
  border, one deforms it while leaving it present. Record both under
  `## Gate probes`.*

### How the zone and its segments are identified, and what that cost

**The zone**, by MC-021's `innermost_rect_containing` - the rect containing the
zone's caption that is smallest by area - with **one change this story's frames
forced**. The idle zone paints one rect (its fill); the drop-hovered zone paints
*two with identical bounds*, a fill and a stroke-only sibling (`rect_filled`
then `rect_stroke`). MC-021's uniqueness guard, which was written to return one
*shape*, fails on that tie with "2 are tied at 56640 sq pt". The version here
requires the tie to be unambiguous about **geometry** - every shape tied for
smallest must have the same `rect` - and returns all of them, which is what
AC-4 needs ("exactly one of the zone's rects carries a stroke"). The nesting
guard is kept verbatim. `tests/palette.rs` is not edited: MC-021 never paints a
hovered frame and does not need the change.

**The paint-list walk** cost three lines to extend, because `Shape::LineSegment`
is a plain struct variant carrying `[Pos2; 2]` and a `Stroke` and needs no
unpacking, unlike the galley MC-021 had to reach into. What did *not*
generalise, exactly as MC-021's handoff predicted, is the identification
helper: `innermost_rect_containing` is blind to anything that is not a
`Shape::Rect`, so it found the zone's fill and contributed nothing at all to
finding the border.

**The border**, therefore, by geometry: a segment belongs to the zone when both
endpoints lie inside the zone's rect generously expanded. The guard is what
makes that safe - **every** segment in the frame must qualify, so a border
painted somewhere else cannot leave the filter empty and let AC-2, AC-3 and
AC-5 pass vacuously over nothing.

### The subtlety AC-5 turns on

The number of `LineSegment`s is **not** the number of dashes.
`dashes_from_line` (`epaint-0.36.2/src/shapes/shape.rs:528`) walks the polyline
one straight piece at a time, and a dash that runs past a polyline vertex is
split into two segments that share an endpoint (`shapes.push(...)` at :573).
This border's corners therefore arrive as runs of two to four pieces with a
**zero** gap between them, and the walker also emits genuinely zero-length
segments where a dash ends exactly on a vertex (the idle frame has one, at
`[488.0 24.5]`). Measuring "the gap between consecutive segments" would measure
a crowd of zeros and report a mean near zero for a border whose gaps are all
4 pt. So segments that share an endpoint are glued back into one dash first:
**134 segments merge into 117 dashes**, and the control test asserts that this
merge is not vacuous rather than trusting the comment.

The seam where the closed polyline's dash pattern fails to come out even - the
last dash ends about 1 pt short of the first - is a quantisation artefact and is
out of scope by name, so gaps are measured between consecutive dashes and
deliberately not wrapped around.

### What is deliberately not asserted

Not the number of dashes, not the coordinates of any one of them, not the seam.
Not colour beyond the single stroke colour AC-1 names. Not the centring of the
zone's caption (MC-030). Not `stroke_kind` or the `CornerRadius` egui records on
the hovered rect, which are a request to the tessellator rather than a painted
position. No renderer, no reference image, no feature flag, no `#[ignore]`.

### One vacuous check, found in review

`boundary_problems` - the check behind
`every_dash_endpoint_lies_on_the_rounded_boundary_and_none_in_a_square_corner` -
originally had **no empty guard**. Over an empty segment list its loop ran zero
times, `worst` stayed `None`, no square-corner point was found, and it returned
no problems: the test reported success when the border had been **deleted
entirely**. The first draft of `### AC-6` below described that as passing
"vacuously ... correctly". It is not correct. AC-3's wording is "when **every**
dash endpoint is tested", and a universally quantified test that reports success
having tested nothing is the assertion `rules.md`'s non-negotiables exist to
prevent. That AC-1 and AC-2 also catch the same mutation is what makes AC-6
satisfiable; it is not a licence for this test to stay silent.

Corrected by adding a guard in the same shape as `inset_problems`' own:

```rust
if segments.is_empty() {
    problems.push(
        "the drop zone paints no boundary at all, so there are no painted endpoints to test \
         against the rounded rectangle `components.md` gives it"
            .to_string(),
    );
    return problems;
}
```

No tolerance moved, no other check moved, no existing control moved, and the
file still has seven tests.

**And the guard has a control of its own**, because a guard nothing exercises is
the same defect one level down. Control 6 in
`the_boundary_checks_reject_a_wrong_inset_square_corners_and_a_reversed_corner_sweep`
covered AC-1, AC-2 and AC-5 over an empty segment list; it now covers AC-3 too:

### The three numbers AC-5 asked to be recorded

Printed by the run, not copied from a comment:

```
AC-5 measured: painted 702 pt / boundary 1167.1239 pt = share 0.6014786;
               134 segments merged into 117 dashes;
               gaps min 3.931509 mean 3.9978948 max 4
```

| Quantity | Measured | Band |
|---|---|---|
| total painted length | **702 pt** | - |
| boundary length | **1167.1239 pt** | - |
| their ratio | **0.6014786** | 0.54 to 0.66 |
| gap between consecutive dashes | min **3.9315**, mean **3.9979**, max **4.0** | 3.6 to 4.4 |

The boundary length is the perimeter of the ideal rounded rectangle,
`2(w-2r) + 2(h-2r) + 2*pi*r` on the inset rect at r = 7.5, computed from the
tokens rather than measured off the polyline. The painter walks a chord
approximation of those arcs, which is 0.14 pt shorter over the whole border -
0.012 %, a rounding error against a 10 % band. Gaps run slightly under 4
because a gap that crosses a corner is measured as a straight line across a
chord polyline, not along it; the worst case is 1.7 % low, well inside.

### The tolerances, and where each came from

| Check | Tolerance | Derivation | Nearest thing it must reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC-2, the inset | **1e-3 pt** | Not from what the code prints. `dashes_from_line` places endpoints at `start + vector * t`, and on each straight edge one component of `vector` is exactly zero, so every endpoint on that edge carries the inset rect's coordinate *bit-identically*. The extent is not near the inset rect, it **is** the inset rect; the only slack needed is the f32 subtraction in the comparison. | `stroke.width % 2.0` displaces every side by **0.5 pt** - 500x this tolerance. The criterion's own 0.5 pt ceiling would have put that mutant exactly *on* the assertion boundary, which is why it is not used. |
| AC-3, on the boundary | **0.06516 pt** = `7.5 * (1 - cos 7.5 deg) + 1e-3` | Six chords per quarter circle, so each subtends 15 degrees and a point on a chord is inside the true arc by at most the sagitta at its midpoint, `r(1 - cos(theta/2))` = 0.06416 at r = 7.5. The `+ 1e-3` is f32 slack. | the square corner, at `r(sqrt(2)-1)` = **3.1066 pt**, 47.7x. The thinnest real mutant is radius 7.0 at **0.2071 pt**, 3.2x. |
| AC-5, both clauses | **10 %**, relative | The criterion's own number, applied to 0.6 (so 0.54 to 0.66) and to 4 pt (so 3.6 to 4.4). | a solid outline at **0.99988**, and a 4-on-6-off pattern at **0.40099** with 5.994 pt gaps. |

**The AC-3 margin is thin on purpose and worth understanding before anyone
widens it.** The shipped border's furthest endpoint is 0.06416 pt out against a
limit of 0.06516 - 98 % of the bound. That is the derivation working rather than
luck: the sagitta *is* the maximum a chord can dip below its arc, and over 24
chords some endpoint lands near a midpoint, so the measured worst case converges
on the analytic bound from below. f32 error in the distance function is around
1e-5 and the `+ 1e-3` covers it a hundredfold. Rounding the tolerance up to 0.1
"for safety" would let a radius of 7.25 through.
