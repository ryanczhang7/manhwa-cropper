## Context

**The bug.** The page column locator trims the outer columns of dark,
low-texture artwork as if they were page background, so the crop cuts into
the art. It was found by the held-out zero-clip check at the end of MC-049's
GATES (MC-049 `## Notes`, "Held-out zero-clip check"). v1 already did it at
margin 3, cutting 2 to 6 px on three entries, and the margin absorbed part of
the cut. At MC-049's margin 0 the cut shows at full depth. The user and the
orchestrator looked at the edges on 2026-09-24:

| Entry | Side | Columns cut at margin 0 | What they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2025-07-17 14_41_58.png` | left | 931–936 | dark purple textured art |
| `2025-07-17 14_41_58.png` | right | 1604–1611 | the same art |
| `2025-07-17 14_55_10.png` | right | 1594–1598 | dark red art (chains) |
| `Screenshot (73).png` | left | 1007–1015 | dark scene art |

**The mechanism, measured by the Lead PO on 2026-09-24 against `26eddcb`
(= `main`, MC-048 merged).** The rule MC-027 built is `page_column` in
`crates/core/src/flat.rs`. It keeps the widest run of columns whose spread is
at or above `min_line_spread` (8.0). Spread is the mean absolute deviation
over the central band of rows. The orchestrator's hypothesis was that the
art's outer columns fall below 8.0, and that holds, in two forms:

- **Low spread at the art's edge.** On `14_41_58` left, columns 928–935 have
  spread 1.9–4.2. On `14_55_10` right, 1594–1617 have spread 4.7–7.3. On `(73)`
  left, 1003–1015 have spread 5.8–7.8.
- **A run broken inside the art.** On `14_41_58` right, 1604–1605 dip to 6.8
  and 7.5, while 1606–1612 climb back to 8.4–14.5. The widest run stops at
  1603 and leaves out the art beyond the dip.

In every case the page background beside the art reads spread **0.0** on
these PNGs. By MC-049's page-background predicate, measured over the mark's
rows, it scores share **1.00**, and the art columns score **0.73–0.89**. The
two are cleanly separable on these files. The difficulty is doing that
without also taking in page columns elsewhere (`## Notes`, "The probe").
