## Context

MC-019 is blocked. The column axis is solved (MC-027; 20 of 21 within 10 px on
this tree) and the row axis carries the entire deficit: the shipped detector
overshoots every row edge by 97 to 310 px, never clipping. MC-028 asked whether
a **panel gutter** is locatable from pixels and came back a reasoned negative -
seven rule shapes, 264 parameterisations, best 4 of 21 with 6 clips, and a
cherry-picked-per-file ceiling of 7 of 21 that no single rule can reach.

**This story asks a different question, and the difference is the whole point.**
MC-028 searched for the boundary between two *panels*. The orchestrator's
reproduction of its escalation (MC-019 `## Notes`) found that what the detector
actually leaves in the output is not extra panel gutter but **browser and OS
chrome**: `Screenshot (2698).jpg` keeps a strip of the URL bar
(`eed-manhwa/chapter-406/`), and `2025-08-05 00_11_13.webp` - the *best* of the
21 at +99/+110 - keeps **two rows of browser tabs** at the top and the **Windows
taskbar** at the bottom. Chrome is fixed-position, full-screen-width UI. A panel
gutter is not. They are different problems and only one of them has been
searched.

### The specific gap this exists to close

MC-028 section 5d reports the column-structure direction - "the pixels *beside*
the page column" - at **0 of 21 at every one of 48 parameterisations**, and
explains it: "the page column is narrower than the browser's toolbars, so the
pixels beside it are flat everywhere in the rect, chrome rows included."

That explanation does not survive inspection of the numbers it rests on. Those
pixels were profiled **inside the pipeline rect**, and the pipeline rect on this
tree is 2 to 10 px wider than the page column (MC-019 `## Notes`, measurement 1:
worst column overshoot per entry is `[4,5,5,6,6,6,6,7,7,8,8,9,9,10,10,10,10,10,
10,10,20]`). There were almost no pixels beside the column to read. Over the
**full image width** the margins do carry signal. **That is not a rule and it is
not a promise**; it is the evidence that the direction is *untested* rather than
ruled out, which is why this is a spike and not a feature story.

### Where to start, so this does not retrace ground

Six measurements are already taken. **Read them before designing anything.**

1. **MC-026 finding 4** - per-row flat fraction separates a gutter row from a
   panel row well: median 0.201 inside a panel, 0.984 in the gutter band, one
   threshold at 0.40 classifying 61 of 63 bands.
2. **MC-026 finding 5** - and it does not locate the page: 0 of 21, 17 to 20
   clips, over every threshold x depth x selector combination.
3. **MC-026 findings 1 and 1b** - mean absolute deviation 5 of 21, the earlier
   flat-fraction attempt 8 of 21. **These two are the baselines to beat.**
4. **MC-028 section 5f, the ordinal finding, and it is the real obstacle** - the
   marked edges *are* at pixel features (40 of 42 within 11 px of a change of
   one of sixteen kinds, 30 of 42 exactly on one), but each feature kind changes
   at 2 to 150 other rows in the same rect, and the ordinal of the right one
   runs k = 1..16, 3..11, 5..35, 6..30 depending on the kind. Separating a
   gutter row from a panel row is easy; **choosing among dozens of correct
   separations is what nothing has managed.** Any rule proposed here must say
   what ranks its candidates.
5. **MC-019 `## Notes` measurement 3** - "the marked edge sits at an art
   boundary" is **vacuous**: 78.6% of marked edges are within 0 px of one, but
   so are 99.4% of all other rows. Do not re-derive it, and do not build a
   metric whose control you have not run.
6. **MC-019 `## Notes` measurement 2** - MC-028's "identical-structure pair
   marked 57 px apart" does not hold; the two pages genuinely differ below the
   shared chrome edge at row 166. **The manifest is not to be treated as broken
   and is not to be edited.**

## Acceptance criteria

The deliverable is one document, `docs/wiki/chrome-row-search.md`.

- **AC-1** — *The question is answered either way.* Given the investigation has ended, when the document is read, then its first section states one of exactly two verdicts: **a recommended rule**, with its corpus score and its clip count under MC-019's window; or **a reasoned negative** - no rule tried reaches the bar - with the alternatives tried and why each failed. **A negative is a real, acceptable and valuable outcome.** It is the answer that stops a fourth attempt at the row axis, and it sends MC-019's numbers back to the user with the chrome-peeling direction closed as well as the gutter one.
- **AC-2** — *The score is stated the way MC-019 will measure it.* Given either verdict, when the document reports a number, then it reports **both** of MC-019's numbers for every rule it scores: how many of the 21 crop entries land inside the expected rect expanded by `margin_px + 8` px, **and** how many are clipped. A rule that scores well and clips has failed. Scores are computed over the final margin-expanded clamped rect, as MC-028 section 5b does, so no `margin_px` convention can drift between documents.
- **AC-3** — *The prior measurements are not re-run as discoveries.* Given the document, when its evidence section is read, then it begins from the six items in `## Context` by reference, and every rule it proposes is compared against the three baselines - 5 of 21, 8 of 21, and 0 of 21 with 17 to 20 clips. A rule that does not beat them is reported as not beating them. Any rule whose candidates are separated but not **ranked** is reported against item 4 explicitly.
- **AC-4** — *The negative controls are stated, and they fire.* Given any rule the document recommends or scores, when its section is read, then it names the inputs the rule must **not** fire on and what it scores on them, and demonstrates each control firing for at least one parameterisation rather than passing vacuously: at minimum (a) a panel containing a flat full-width row, (b) the seven `"expect": "flag"` corpus entries, whose no-regression baseline is **1 of 7 already cropped** (`2025-02-27 22_46_15.png`), and (c) a page with **no** browser chrome at all - a rule keyed to chrome must not invent an edge where there is none. A rule with no control is reported as having none.
- **AC-5** — *Every number is reproducible.* Given the document, when a number in it is questioned, then the document names the command that produced it and the location of the harness, as MC-025's, MC-026's and MC-028's `## Notes` do. No number appears without one.
- **AC-6** — *The next story is specified or explicitly not needed.* Given a recommended rule, when the document ends, then it states the shape of the implementation story: which module, which `Tuning` field if any, the required gate that would fail if it broke, and whether it fits one RED -> GREEN cycle. Given a reasoned negative, it states instead what it would take for MC-019 to be completable at all, given that the tolerance is fixed and the manifest is not to be edited - including, if that is the honest answer, that MC-019 should be split by axis or descoped, and what evidence the user would need to decide.

### The answer

**No.** The full-width chrome signal locates the **browser viewport** - the row
the chrome ends at and the row the taskbar begins at - cleanly, unambiguously
and without ever clipping, and the viewport is not what the manifest marks:
scored against the marks it is **0 of 21 with 0 clips**, and the best rule of
1312 parameterisations over twelve rule shapes is **5 of 21 with 5 clips**
(best clip-free 1 of 21, best top-rule x bottom-rule hybrid 6 of 21). Nothing
beats MC-026's 8 of 21. A reasoned negative, and the residual it leaves is
panel gutter - MC-028's closed question.

One finding is worth more than the score: under the chrome rule **every output
is free of browser and OS chrome**, which is the defect MC-019 `## Notes`
measurement 4 raised. The deliverable's section 9 puts that back to the user as
a decision rather than a story.
