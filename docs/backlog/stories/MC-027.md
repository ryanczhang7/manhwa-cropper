## Context

Half of the locator problem is solved and has been for two stories; nobody has
shipped it because it was measured only as a by-product of a rule that failed on
the other axis.

A reader screenshot has a **page margin** on each side - the flat browser
background to the left and right of the page column - and a **panel gutter**
between panels, which is a different thing with a different detector
(`docs/wiki/architecture.md`, "Two kinds of blank space"). This story is the
page margin only. MC-028 is the panel gutter, and the row axis stays exactly
where MC-025 left it.

**The measurement that makes this a `feature` and not a spike** is MC-026's
finding 7. Starting from the rect the **real** pipeline hands the flat stage -
`trim_uniform` -> `content_box` -> `trim_within`, no marked rect anywhere - and
measuring column flatness over a **central band of that rect's rows** rather
than all of them, the widest textured band places the page's left and right
edges inside MC-019's window on **19 of 21** corpus entries:

```
central fraction of the rect's rows    1.0    0.8    0.6    0.5    0.4   0.25
widest textured band                     2     19     19     18     18     16
outermost textured columns               0     15     15     12     13     14
```

Three things in that table are the whole story. The central-band restriction is
what makes it work at all (1.0 scores 2, because the browser chrome and taskbar
rows that survive inside the rect are textured out to column 0 - MC-025's
diagnosis, from the other side). The **widest-band** selector beats
`textured_span`'s **outermost** rule by four entries, where on the row axis
every selector scored identically. And the parameter has a real window, 0.6 to
0.8, unlike the bracket depth of MC-026's retired premise, which was inert at
every value.

**The hard part is the same one MC-025 hit**, and it is where this story's
engineering content is:

1. `flat::narrow` stands down on any axis where `strong_lines` found anything,
   and on a real screenshot it always has. Two frozen assertions in
   `crates/core/tests/decide.rs` enforce that guard - MC-025's orchestrator
   confirmed both by mutation - so it cannot simply be deleted. Read MC-025's
   `## Notes`, "GREEN: the wiring, and the alternatives the frozen suite
   rejected", before writing a line: it lists four wirings and the exact
   assertion that killed each.
2. `edges::textured_span` takes the **outermost** textured indices and does not
   stop at a flat run between them. That is deliberate - it is how MC-005
   decision 13 is honoured - and it is why the column axis scores 15 rather than
   19. Adding a selector is in scope; changing what `textured_span` means is
   not.

`Screenshot (1661).png` is the known awkward file: a second textured region to
the right of the page. MC-025 recorded it as a `+1086 px` error under the
outermost rule and MC-026 finding 7 records it resolving correctly, `-4,+3`,
under a widest-band rule from the pipeline's own rect. It is a case, not an
obstacle.

Gate that fails if this breaks: `unit` (required) for the locator itself, and
`integration` for AC-4 and AC-5, which are corpus criteria - `integration` is
**optional for the repo**, so it is named in `required_gates` above. AC-4 and
AC-5 are `#[ignore]`d tests in `crates/engine/tests/corpus.rs`, which MC-026
creates; add to that target rather than making a second one.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** — Given a `Luma` plane, a rect and a `Tuning`, when the column locator runs, then it measures each column's spread over only a **central band** of the rect's rows, whose height is `Tuning`'s central-band fraction of the rect's height, centred on the rect - and a column outside that band's rows cannot change the answer. *Pinned by construction:* a fixture with art in the central band and full-width texture in the top and bottom sixths of the rect must give the same answer as the same fixture with those sixths blanked.
- **AC-2** — Given a column spread profile with two textured runs separated by a flat run, when the locator selects, then it returns the **widest** run and not the span from the first to the last. *Contrast with `edges::textured_span`, which is required to keep returning the outermost pair on the same profile* - both in one test, because the difference between them is what buys the four entries.
- **AC-3** — Given a fixture whose page column sits inside flat page margins, when `decide` runs at `Tuning::default()`, then the result is `Crop(rect)` whose rect excludes both page margins beyond `margin_px` and contains the full width of the page column, where before this story the same fixture yields a rect spanning the whole image width.
- **AC-4** — *The corpus, on the column axis only.* Given every corpus entry with an expected rect, when `process_file` runs at `Tuning::default()`, then the produced rect's **left and right** edges lie within MC-019's window of the expected rect's - `[e.x - 8, e.x + 3]` and `[e.x + e.w - 1 - 3, e.x + e.w - 1 + 8]` - on at least **19 of 21** entries. The test prints a per-file table of both offsets. Rows are explicitly not measured by this criterion; they are MC-028's. *Control on the metric:* with the expected rect's left edge shifted 40 px inward before comparison, the check must fail on at least 15 entries - a test that passes whatever the locator returns is the failure mode here, and the measured offsets are all within 7 px.
- **AC-5** — *Zero clips, still.* Given every corpus entry with an expected rect, when `process_file` runs, then no entry whose outcome is `Cropped { rect, .. }` has a `rect` that fails to contain the expected rect entirely. The count is **0** before this story. **One file is known to be at risk and it is 2 px:** `2026-01-05 13_45_59.png` is located `1039..1505` against a marked `1040..1510`, which after `margin_px` is `1036..1508`. If the implementation reproduces that, **stop** - do not widen the margin, do not special-case the file, and do not edit the manifest. Report it, and the orchestrator opens the image with the user and records which of the two is wrong (MC-019's `## Model guidance` is the procedure).
- **AC-6** — Given the workspace suite, when it runs, then every assertion still holds: **361 pass on `main` at `a42f8e8`, 1 ignored**, plus whatever MC-026 leaves. In particular `no_border_found_beats_ambiguous_on_an_image_that_is_both` and `a_detection_with_a_nearly_chrome_edge_strip_is_flagged_ambiguous` in `crates/core/tests/decide.rs` still hold - they are what MC-025's stand-down guard exists for, and loosening the guard is the obvious way to break them.
- **AC-7** — Given the central-band fraction this story introduces, when the story ends, then `docs/wiki/architecture.md`'s tuning table carries it, and `## Notes` carries its derivation: the window it must lie in, the measurement at each end, and the control that fires outside it.

## Model guidance

Model: default. Oracle partition:

- **Settled - read out, do not re-derive**: `min_line_spread` 8.0 and its floor/ceiling derivation (MC-025), `margin_px` 3, MC-005 decision 13, MC-007 AC-6's flag order, whatever MC-026 settles for `min_content_fraction` and `ambiguity_band`, and MC-019's hit window (`margin_px + 8` of slack, which is `[-8, +3]` on a low edge and `[-3, +8]` on a high one). This story adds a selector and a row restriction; it retunes nothing.
- **Oracle-free, and the one place judgement is needed**: the **central-band fraction**. The corpus is its oracle and MC-026 finding 7 is evidence, not the answer - the window `[0.6, 0.8]` is measured on 21 files at one threshold with one selector, and the value should be re-derived with its own controls. MC-025's derivation of `min_line_spread` is the standard: a floor that fires, a ceiling that fires, a corpus-backed value between them, and a test that tells a later story loudly when it tries to leave the window. `Screenshot (2708).png` is the file that fires at the low end (`+1,+7` at 0.6, `+85,+7` at 0.5); `1.0` is the file-independent ceiling, where the score collapses from 19 to 2.
- **Mechanical**: AC-1, AC-2, AC-6, AC-7, and the per-file tables.

**Where to spend the thinking.** Not on the statistic - it is MC-025's, shipped
and measured. On the **wiring**: when does the column locator get to speak, given
that `flat::narrow`'s stand-down guard is load-bearing for two frozen tests and
unconditional on real screenshots? MC-025's `## Notes` lists four wirings and the
assertion that rejected each; a fifth is what this story needs, and the frozen
suite is the arbiter, exactly as it was there.

**Sizing.** One RED -> GREEN cycle: one behaviour - the page column's left and
right edges are found - with one new `Tuning` field, one new selector and one
wiring decision. It is a *large* one cycle, comparable to MC-025. If RED finds
it cannot be stated without also changing what `textured_span` means, that is the
signal to stop and split, not to widen the story.

## Out of scope

**The row axis, entirely.** This story does not locate a panel gutter, does not
change `flat`'s row behaviour, and does not improve MC-019's whole-rect metric -
after it lands, most corpus entries will still be cropped to something far too
tall, and that is the expected outcome. MC-028 owns the rows.

Changing what `edges::textured_span` returns (the outermost pair) or the
`min_line_spread` threshold. Retuning any `Tuning::default()` field other than
the one this story adds. Colour or chroma signals - measured and ruled out in
MC-025's `## Context`. Per-reader special cases. Changing the corpus files or the
manifest, including the 2 px question in AC-5, which is the user's to answer.
Meeting the 90% bar, which stays MC-019's.
