## Context

**This story was re-scoped after its own investigation.** It began as an
interior-band locator; the measurement retired that premise and found a larger
blocker underneath it, which is what the story is now about. The investigation
is preserved in full under `## Notes` and is the evidence for everything below.

**The blocker.** Feed `decide` a *perfect* locator - each corpus entry's
hand-marked rect, expanded by `margin_px` - and at `Tuning::default()` it
returns `Crop` on **2 of 21**. Eleven are `Flag(LowContent)` because
`min_content_fraction` is 0.20 and every marked rect in the corpus is below
0.2113, the smallest being 0.0802 of its image. Eight more are `Flag(Ambiguous)`
from `content_box` before the size test is reached. So **no locator story can
move the corpus metric past 2 of 21 while those two constants stand**, and the
corpus cannot judge a locator improvement at all. MC-025's AC-6 measured 0 -> 0
and read it as a search-rule failure; a perfect MC-025 would have measured 2.

A reader page on a 2560x1440 screen is roughly 500 x 1150 px - one sixth of the
screen. `min_content_fraction` was settled on synthetic fixtures where the art
fills most of the frame, and the corpus says it is wrong by more than a factor
of two. `ambiguity_band` was settled the same way, and the corpus says the same
thing about it: every one of the eight ambiguous entries clears at a band of
0.0025 or less (`## Notes`, finding 6).

**Whose constants these are.** `architecture.md`'s tuning table and MC-019's
`## Context` reserved every `Tuning::default()` change to MC-019, and MC-025
and this story's own stub both put retuning out of scope. **The user has
narrowed that reservation**: `min_content_fraction` and `ambiguity_band` are
re-settled here, because MC-019 cannot measure anything until they are.
Everything else in the table stays MC-019's. `architecture.md` and MC-019's
`## Context` are updated to say so; this paragraph and those two are the same
decision written three times, deliberately, so none of them contradicts the
others.

This story changes no locator and adds no detection technique. It moves two
numbers and proves, on the corpus, that the decision path stops discarding
pages it should keep - with the seven `"expect": "flag"` entries as the control
that stops it from simply saying yes to everything.

Gate that fails if this breaks: `integration` (`cargo test --workspace
--release -- --ignored`), which is **optional for the repo** and therefore named
in `required_gates` above; and `unit`, which runs the constant pins and the
fixtures built around them.

## Superseded: the stub's premise

The stub's premise was MC-025's closing diagnosis: `edges::textured_span`
returns the *outermost* indices at or above `Tuning::min_line_spread`, a real
screenshot is textured out to its edges, so the span is the whole rect on every
corpus file, and what is needed instead is a search for an **interior** band -
a run of textured lines bracketed by flat runs. That premise was tested against
all 28 corpus screenshots, across five selection rules, twelve bracket depths
and eleven flatness thresholds. Three things came out of it, in increasing
order of how much they matter.

**1. The interior-band rule works on one axis and not the other.** Given the
rows for free - clipped to the hand-marked rect - a band rule places the page's
left and right edges inside MC-019's window on **19 of 21** entries, at every
depth and every threshold tried. Given the columns for free, a band rule places
the top and bottom on **5 of 21**; cherry-picking the best selector, depth
*and* threshold per file, still only **7 of 21**. A two-axis search is capped
by the worse axis, so the rule family's ceiling on the corpus is 5 to 7 of 21
where MC-019 needs 20 of 21.

> **WRONG, corrected 2026-09-14.** This paragraph originally continued: *"what
> sits above and below the artwork is not gutter, it is reader UI, and dark
> text on a uniform background has a mean absolute deviation of 25 to 65
> against a threshold of 8."* The second half is a true measurement; the first
> half is false. The user confirmed that the content above and below a marked
> rect is **panel gutter** - white or black space between panels, sometimes
> with a speech bubble on it - and not reader UI. What the deviation figure
> actually shows is that a **mean absolute deviation cannot see a panel
> gutter**, because a speech bubble destroys it; the median-based flat fraction
> can, and does (finding 4). See `## Notes`, "The correction".

**2. The rule trades clips for crops, and a clip is the worst defect there
is.** `Screenshot (93).jpg` is one page of two panels with an 18 px gutter
between them; a bracket depth of 18 or less splits it and a widest-band rule
then crops one panel. Measured through the whole pipeline at
`Tuning::default()`, band rules produce up to **2 clips** where MC-025's
outermost rule produces **zero** - and once the tuning gates of finding 3 are
opened so that correct crops are not thrown away before the comparison, **3 to
11 clips**.
That is not a tuning accident: MC-005's decision 13 keeps every panel rather
than cutting at the largest, and `textured_span`'s outermost rule is how that
decision is honoured. An interior-band search is in direct tension with it.

**3. The binding constraint is not the search rule at all.** Feed `decide` the
hand-marked rect itself - a *perfect* locator, expanded by `margin_px` - and at
`Tuning::default()` it crops **2 of 21**. Eleven are flagged `LowContent`
because `min_content_fraction` is 0.20 and **every** marked rect in the corpus
is below 0.2113, the smallest being 0.0802 of its image. Eight more are flagged
`Ambiguous` by `content_box` before the area test is even reached. So **no
locator story can move the corpus metric past 2 of 21** while those two
constants stand, which also explains MC-025's 0 -> 0 more completely than
MC-025's own diagnosis did: a perfect MC-025 would have measured 2.

`Screenshot (1661).png`, which the stub called out as the awkward file, turns
out **not** to be a problem: the +1086 px error was an artefact of the
outermost rule. Once the rows are clipped, the second textured region to its
right is a separate band and the page is the one chosen - located x 1073..1473
against a marked 1077..1470. Its *rows* are unreachable, like everyone else's.

**The three questions this section used to hold are answered.** Q1 - the corpus
marks a boundary that exists in the pixels: it is a **panel gutter**, and
finding 4 separates it from artwork on 61 of 63 bands. Q2 - the user narrowed
MC-019's reservation to hand `min_content_fraction` and `ambiguity_band` to
this story. Q3 - this story became the tuning story; the column locator is
MC-027 and the panel-gutter search is MC-028.

## Acceptance criteria

The oracle is *given every corpus entry's marked rect, `decide` returns `Crop`*.
`decide` takes an image and not a rect, so that one sentence is AC-1, AC-2 and
AC-3 below - the three questions `decide` asks in order, each asked of the
public API. Together they are the whole of it: nothing else stands between a
marked rect and `Crop`.

The corpus criteria are checked by `#[ignore]`d tests in
**`crates/engine/tests/corpus.rs`** - the target MC-019's `## Context` names and
which does not exist yet, so this story creates it - run by the **`integration`**
gate in release mode. That is why `integration` is in `required_gates`. Note
`crates/engine/tests/corpus_manifest.rs` already exists and is a different
thing: it checks the corpus is *well formed*, is not `#[ignore]`d, and is not
where these belong.

- **AC-1** - *The size gate admits every marked page.* Given every corpus entry with an expected rect, when that rect is expanded by `margin_px` and clamped to the image, then its area is at least `min_content_fraction` of the image's and both of its sides are at least `min_content_side`. The test prints a per-file table of area fractions and names the first entry that fails. *Control on the constant:* the same check applied to every marked rect **with both sides divided by three** - one ninth of the area, 0.0089 to 0.0235 of its image - must **fail on every entry**. A `min_content_fraction` low enough to admit those is not a floor at all. The admissible window is therefore `(0.0235, 0.0802]`, measured; pick inside it and record how.
- **AC-2** - *No marked page is ambiguous.* Given every corpus entry with an expected rect, when `detect` runs at `Tuning::default()`, then `Detection::ambiguous` is `false`. Eight are `true` today, named in finding 6. *Control on the constant:* the ambiguity path must still fire at `Tuning::default()` - a strip whose flat fraction sits inside the new band below `chrome_flat_fraction` still makes `detect` report `ambiguous: true`, and one below the band still does not. An `ambiguity_band` of 0.0 satisfies AC-2 by deleting the feature and fails this control. The measured window is `(0, 0.0025]`.
- **AC-3** - *No marked page is answered `NoBorderFound`.* Given every corpus entry with an expected rect, when `detect` runs at `Tuning::default()`, then `trimmed` is true or `removed` is non-empty, so `decide`'s first question does not pre-empt the other two. This holds on `main` at `a42f8e8` for all 21; it is here because AC-1 and AC-2 are worthless without it, and because a tuning change that stopped the pipeline trimming would satisfy both of them and still answer `Flag`.
- **AC-4** - *The negative control: flagged pages stay flagged.* Given the seven `"expect": "flag"` corpus entries, when `process_file` runs at `Tuning::default()`, then the six that are `Flagged` on `main` at `a42f8e8` are still `Flagged`: `2025-03-03 11_06_04.png`, `2025-03-03 11_24_19.png`, `2025-05-12 22_55_40.png`, `2025-05-12 22_58_53.png`, `Screenshot (3455).png`, `Screenshot (3465).png` - all six by `Detector(NoBorderFound)`. The seventh, `2025-02-27 22_46_15.png`, is cropped to `0,3 553x853` today and this story is not required to fix it; that is MC-019's 90%.
- **AC-5** - *Zero clips, still.* Given every corpus entry with an expected rect, when `process_file` runs, then no entry whose outcome is `Cropped { rect, .. }` has a `rect` that fails to contain the expected rect entirely. The count is **0 on `main` at `a42f8e8`** and opening a size gate is exactly the change that could turn a flag into a clip.
- **AC-6** - *Nothing else moves.* Given the workspace suite, when it runs, then every assertion still holds except the pins and fixtures built around the two old values, each named in the handoff with its old and new expectation and the reason it had to move. **361 tests pass on `main` at `a42f8e8`, 1 ignored.** A test deleted or `#[ignore]`d instead of rebuilt is a return to RED, not a pass.
- **AC-7** - Given the two constants changed, when the story ends, then `docs/wiki/architecture.md`'s tuning table carries the new values and `## Notes` carries the before/after corpus figures for AC-1 to AC-5.

## Model guidance

harness named there is still on disk and every number in this story can be
re-run in minutes.

Oracle partition:

- **Settled - read out, do not re-derive**: `margin_px` 3, `min_content_side` 64, `chrome_flat_fraction` 0.85, `min_line_spread` 8.0, `uniform_tolerance` 10, `edge_threshold` 24, `min_content_stddev` 12, `chrome_max_extent` 0.30; MC-007 AC-6's flag order; and the containment predicate in AC-5. None of these is this story's to touch.
- **Oracle-free, and the two places judgement is needed**: `min_content_fraction` and `ambiguity_band`. The corpus is the oracle for both, and MC-025's derivation of `min_line_spread` is the standard to match - a floor from a control that fires, a ceiling from a measurement, a value between them with evidence, and the whole derivation written down. The windows in `## Notes` finding 6 are evidence, not the answer: re-derive them, do not copy them. A value at either end of its window is a value with no margin, and the story should say why it is safe there.
- **Mechanical**: AC-3, AC-6, AC-7, and the per-file tables.

**What RED has to know before it starts.** Both constants are pinned by
exact-equality assertions - `the_default_min_content_fraction_is_zero_point_two`
in `crates/core/tests/decide.rs` and
`the_default_ambiguity_band_is_zero_point_zero_five` in
`crates/core/tests/content.rs` - and both have **fixtures built around the old
value** that will change answer, not merely expectation:
`a_rect_below_min_content_fraction_of_the_image_is_flagged_low_content` (19% of
the image) and `a_rect_at_exactly_min_content_fraction_is_not_below_it_and_is_cropped`
in `decide.rs`, and the 0.84-flat-fraction strips in `content.rs` and
`decide.rs`. Correcting a pin in RED is a genuine watched failure - the source
still holds the old value, so the corrected assertion is red on its first run.
Correcting a *fixture* is not: rebuild it so that the scene still sits where
the test's name says it sits, and earn it the way `rules.md` requires, with a
mutation that turns the one assertion red. Grep first; the list above is what
was visible from PLANNED and is not promised to be complete.

## Out of scope

Any locator change, any new detection technique, and any wiring change in
`decide` or `detect` - this story moves two numbers. Changing `decide`'s flag
order (MC-007 AC-6). Changing any other `Tuning::default()` field; those stay
MC-019's, and `min_content_side` in particular does not bind here (the smallest
marked side is 246 px). Colour or chroma signals - measured and ruled out in
MC-025's `## Context`. Per-reader special cases. Changing the corpus files or
the manifest. Meeting the 90% bar, which stays MC-019's: this story will leave
most entries cropped to something far too large, and that is the expected
outcome, not a failure.

## Test plan

Two places, because the criteria live in two places.

**The corpus criteria (AC-1 to AC-5)** go in a new target,
`crates/engine/tests/corpus.rs` - the file MC-019's `## Context` names and
which did not exist. Seven `#[ignore]`d tests, run by the **`integration`**
gate. They are integration tests because the contract they check is between
the detector and twenty-eight real files: no unit can falsify "the size gate
admits a real reader page", and no end-to-end run is needed to. Every one
accumulates its violations over the whole corpus and asserts once, with a
per-file table in the message and the first failing entry named.

| `a_marked_page_with_both_sides_divided_by_three_is_rejected_by_the_size_gate` | AC-1 control | the same check on the same rects with both sides divided by three must fail on every one - the floor of the window |

Out of scope stays out: nothing here constrains a locator, a search rule or the

### The two constants, and how they were derived

The full derivation is in the module header of `crates/engine/tests/corpus.rs`
and is read out rather than restated in `decide.rs` and `content.rs`. In brief,
and re-derived here rather than copied from finding 6:

**`min_content_fraction = 0.05`.** `cargo run --bin mcf` in the measurement
harness (below) prints the whole table.

* Ceiling **0.080186**, measured: the smallest marked page, `Screenshot
  (93).jpg`, 246x1167 marked, 252x1173 after `margin_px`, in a 2560x1440 frame.
  Above it a real page is rejected. The twenty-one run 0.080186 to 0.211156.
* Floor **0.024082**, from AC-1's control, which fires: the same rects with both
  sides divided by three run 0.009426 to 0.024082, the largest being
  `2025-08-05 00_11_13.webp` at 216x411. At or below it a rect one ninth the
  area of a page is admitted.
* Window `(0.024082, 0.080186]`; **0.05** is 2.08x the floor and 1.60x below the
  ceiling. It is deliberately above the window's geometric centre (0.0439),
  because the defect a too-low value permits is a **clip** and the defect a

  `8000f32 / 160000f32` is bit for bit `0.05f32`, which keeps AC-3's boundary

not a margin, so `cargo run --bin bandexact` bisects the threshold per entry.

* Ceiling **0.00324973**, measured: the binding pair is `2026-01-05 13_45_59.png`
  and `2026-01-05 13_49_39.png`, whose offending strip has flat fraction
  0.846750. The next entry up is `2026-01-05 13_33_41.png` at 0.009935. Swept:

* Window `(0.0016667, 0.0032497)`; **0.0025** is 1.50x the floor, 1.30x below

* It is the **only** value in that window whose foot is a ratio of whole pixels:
  `0.85 - 0.0025 = 0.8475 = 339/400`, and `678f32 / 800f32` and

### What RED discovered that changes the implementation approach

**Nothing about the implementation - it really is two literals - but one thing
about the fixtures that cost an hour and would have cost GREEN the same.**

`common::Chrome` (the `Recipe` generator `decide.rs` uses) draws its "text"
pixels **per line**: `text_per_line(long) = floor((1 - flat) * long)`, the same
count on every line of the band's long axis. So the flat fractions a band of
width `long` can take are exactly the multiples of `1 / long`, and the rounding
is *down*, which for a sub-threshold band pushes it **up** toward
`chrome_flat_fraction`. At the old art width of 200 the step is 0.005 - twice
the whole new `ambiguity_band` - so **no band 200 px wide can land inside
`[0.8475, 0.85)` at all**: every value that tried rounded onto 0.85 exactly and
was peeled as chrome, which is how six unrelated `decide.rs` tests first went
red with "a kept band is never a removal; got [Top]". Widening the art to 800
makes the step 0.00125, half the band, and the requested rungs render exactly.
`content.rs` was never affected because it calls `common::chrome_band` directly,
which distributes over the whole strip (`1 / (w * h)`) rather than per line.

The doc comment on `Chrome::text_per_line` claims the quantisation "can only
ever push it away from `chrome_flat_fraction`, never towards it". That is true
for a band asked for *more* than the threshold and false for one asked for
less. It is left alone here - MC-026 moves two numbers - but it is a real
latent trap and worth a note in whatever story next touches that generator.

Two smaller things:

* `process_file` already crops **13** of the 21 marked entries at
  `Tuning::default()` (to nearly the whole image), so AC-5 is not vacuous in
  RED. At the previewed constants it crops **21** with **0** clips.
* MC-025's recorded "0 of 21" was 0 *correct* crops, not 0 crops. The
  distinction matters when reading AC-5's table.

### The correction: two senses of "gutter", and what it invalidated

Findings 1 to 3 below were measured while the word **gutter** was being used in
two incompatible senses, and two sentences in them are wrong as a result. Both
are corrected in place above rather than deleted, with this section as the
reason.

- **The codebase's sense** - now called **page margin**: the flat area to the
  left and right of the page column. That is what `trim.rs`, MC-025 and
  `architecture.md` meant.
- **The user's sense** - now called **panel gutter**: the white or black space
  *between panels*, running horizontally across the strip. Speech bubbles sit
  on it. It is still gutter and it is still not wanted in the crop.

`architecture.md`, "cropper-core", now defines both and uses them consistently.

**What the user confirmed**, and it is the fact these findings were missing:
the content outside a marked rect *vertically* is **not artwork**. It is panel
gutter. The marks are not drawn by taste; they bound a panel between two real
inter-panel gutters. `Screenshot (103).jpg` is the awkward one - black above
(not strictly a gutter, but to be treated as one) and a **white diagonal
gutter** below - and the user has said explicitly that **103 is not a
priority** and that including the region above it is acceptable if necessary.

Two sentences are therefore withdrawn: "the artwork continues straight through
the line the manifest draws" (`## Superseded`, above) and "there is nothing for
a flatness locator to find there" (finding 1b). Both were inferences from a
mean absolute deviation, and finding 4 is what corrects them.

### A coverage failure that was not the code

The first full `gates.sh` run on this tree reported `FAIL coverage (42s, exit
101)`, and the log holds **no failing test, no panic and no error line** - the
table is complete and `TOTAL` reads 99.71% lines against a `--fail-under-lines`
of 95. What the run does carry, a few lines earlier, is
`Blocking waiting for file lock on build directory`. Re-run alone the gate
passes in 50s, and the full suite then passed all seven.

Recorded because the useful part is how it was diagnosed rather than that it
happened: the summary line said a required gate failed, and the temptation at
that point is to treat 99.02% regions against a 95% *lines* threshold as the
cause and start adjusting something. Reading the log first showed the threshold
was met with four points to spare and that nothing had failed, which is what
pointed at the lock rather than at the story. A gate that fails for a reason
the log does not contain is a gate to re-run, not a number to move.

**Corrected: the cause was not environmental, it was the orchestrator.** The
paragraph above says "re-run it" and stops one question short. The Feature
Developer, resumed to fix a stale comment, was re-running `gates.sh` on this
same working tree at the same moment - because the orchestrator sent it work
and then started a full gate run without waiting for it. Two `gates.sh`
processes shared one `target/` directory, fought over `llvm-cov`'s profraw
files, and then raced to rewrite `## Gate results`, last writer winning. That
is why three runs' worth of mismatched durations appeared interleaved in the
record, and why the *same tree hash* carried both a pass and a fail.

Two things follow, and the second is the one that matters.

1. **The record on this branch is sound**, and it was checked rather than
   assumed: `result: pass` at tree `2b4248021a96…`, which is the hash
   `check-boundaries.sh` independently confirmed against the working tree
   before the PR was opened. A record written by the losing run would have
   failed that check, which is exactly what that check is for.
2. **`gates.sh` takes no lock** - not around `target/`, and not around its own
   rewrite of `## Gate results` in `record_in_story`. So a concurrent run can
   silently replace the record with a verdict against a tree nobody intended,
   and the only thing standing between that and a bad merge is
   `check-boundaries.sh`'s hash comparison. That is a real gap in the harness
   rather than a fact about this story, and it is filed as its own task.

The process lesson is the orchestrator's: **do not start a gate run while a
subagent you dispatched may still be working in the same tree.** The Stop hook
that flagged the mid-story pause caught the symptom; the cause was dispatching
and then not waiting.
